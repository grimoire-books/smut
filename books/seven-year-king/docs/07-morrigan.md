# 7. The Morrígan

From **Planning Bible · Draft 07**. Canonical whole: [PLANNING-BIBLE.md](PLANNING-BIBLE.md). Word source: `material/source/Seven_Year_King_Planning_Bible.docx`.

Locked decisions are marked **LOCK**. Open choices are marked **OPEN**.

The only Irish-myth name for a long stretch. That is deliberate world-building and foreshadowing, per the brief.

> *She never pretends the leash is love.*

That sentence is a design rule for every scene she is in. It is also the book's thesis. The leash is the job. The love is real and separate. She can hold both. Danu cannot. The Shadow King cannot. The nest cannot. That is why she is the only big deity figure who actually loves, and why it takes the whole novel to see it.

**How she works**

- Whispers into the hearts and minds of men. Incites them to battle. Always, she will say, in the service of the Earth Goddess. This is the cover.

- Occasionally raises a great warrior for a great destiny. Speaks to them slowly, gradually, building, until the final night.

- On that night she appears as a woman and fucks them. The next day they die a glorious death, completely her servant.

- They enter a battle frenzy, take many lives, accomplish one strategically essential thing, then succumb. She watches from above in crow form.

- Always their death has meaning. She is a master of strategy. She spends her champions at the best time. Their glory is assured.

**What the others pretend**

- Danu dresses the harvest as marriage. Husband of the land. Warm language. Ripen him, keep him till year 7, burst him into the hoard. The leash is called love.

- The nest and the sire-tree dress hunger as family. Blood-bond, protection, what we take we keep. Sincere feeling inside a collar. The leash is called love.

- The Shadow King dresses immortality as belonging, or as an end to hunger. Keep. Do not lose.

**What she does instead**

She separates the sentences and will not collapse them:

- I will use you.

- You will die.

- I want you.

She never folds 3 into 1 to make 2 easier to swallow. Comfort would be a lie. Comfort is what the others sell. Honesty is the only form her love is allowed to take, which is why she is hard to love until the last page, and why that page is the slow-burn payoff of the entire book.

**Morally correct, not morally pretty**

Greater good = the valve. Both realms fed. No hoard. She does what that requires, always. Correctness is in the paying of her own costs, not in cleanliness. She likes them. She fucks them. She whispers them into knives. She is allowed to be cruel, hungry, tired, fond, even jealous for one uncontrolled flicker. She still will not lie to win him. She does not compete with the nest. She outlasts it.

Love, as this book argues for it: the willingness to lose what you love rather than corrupt it by keeping it. Danu keeps (harvest). The Shadow King keeps (undeath). The Morrígan releases. That is why she can run the valve and they cannot. The valve is the same virtue at the scale of two worlds that the last night is at the scale of two people.

**The champion ethic**

Saying yes to her is not submission-romance. It is the same strength she has --- holding love and death without renaming either. Cú Chulainn refused and still died. The prologue champion walks into morning because he can bear it. That is why she chose him. The seven-year hope Danu sells is the temptation to KEEP. The protagonist's early death is him recovering the champion-virtue: spend, don't hoard. Power does not change the contract. Love does not either. He can become the most powerful being in the universe and still be her champion.

**The slow burn --- recognition, not announcement**

- Prologue: she spends a man. Reader reads predator. Sex is claiming, strategy, want. Leave it sharp. After the last page the scene is newly legible as love. It does not soften. The reader grows the eyes.

- Y0--2: crow, whisper, weather, the dream. She does not appear as a rival girlfriend. She lets the vampires love him in the false way. She does not rescue him from the leash that pretends.

- Near-misses only: washer at a ford, a woman on a night train, a crow on a stone he cannot pass. Every appearance readable two ways.

- The Deal: he marries Danu's language. She is present and silent. She does not correct the seven-year wedding. Correcting it would be a seduction. She refuses the seduction.

- After the cascade: everyone left offers comfort-as-possession. She offers the fact. She stays. She does not say I told you so. That is when he first suspects what she has been.

- Last night: the burn completes. He understands she loved him the entire time and will collect him at dawn. He has the same strength. They bear it together. Sex is the treaty and the love and the briefing. Not a trick.

- Last page, crow: she loved him. She spent him. She can carry that. She flies. The job continues. Not grief-porn. Not a smirk. Not a speech about the greater good. The reader who has walked the book already has the thesis.

**Her faces**

She can be one woman or three. For the prologue, pick a face and commit --- or let her shift across the night. Maiden is a trap. Sovereign is the one who negotiates the treaty. Crone-beautiful is the one who already knows the wound that will kill him. All three across one night is available and should be used at most twice in the book: prologue, finale.

**Rules for writing her**

- Presence is not thesis. She can be on the page. Motive stays hidden until the last night. Plant consistency, not warmth.

- She never lies. She omits. The distinction matters.

- She does not rescue him. She spends him well.

- Do not say the words. I love you is Danu's register and the nest's. Last night can be I will be there / you already knew. Crow-POV can hold the feeling without the sentence.

- Never I wouldn't ask if I didn't love you. That collapses love into an excuse for the leash.

- Do not put the thesis in a witch's mouth in year two. If anyone lectures true love lets go, the ending is spent.

- Narration never sneers at the nest to make her look truer. Vampire loves stay sincere. They failed the losing test, not the feeling test.

- One jealousy flicker, or save it for crow-POV. A subplot of competing for him turns her into a romance heroine in act two.

- Sex grammar: tonight, morning, watch, bear, go. Time words. She never says stay. Never mine. Never harvest.
