# 8. The Horned God and the seven-year deal

From **Planning Bible · Draft 07**. Canonical whole: [PLANNING-BIBLE.md](PLANNING-BIBLE.md). Word source: `material/source/Seven_Year_King_Planning_Bible.docx`.

Locked decisions are marked **LOCK**. Open choices are marked **OPEN**.

**LOCK.** Two clocks, only one advertised. Seven years is the expectation at the pledge --- his, and the reader's. His death is not seven years in. Do not signal that the clock can break. The prologue champion appeared to die when his death meant something; that pattern sells the idea that this man will get his full term. He will not.

**What he signs**

Danu's geis, spoken as sovereignty rite, not as mechanics: power now, seven years as consort and weapon, then he goes into the earth for her. He names a real end-date at the pledge --- a Samhain, a solstice. He makes a concrete plan that requires year six: a place, a person, a winter he thinks he will still have. The mid-tree lover(s) believe the date too. They plan a life inside his term.

What the pledge language must not say: that the Horned God-body is a vessel designed to ripen for seven years and then burst into the land. That bursting-vessel truth is what he understands on the last night. If the pledge explains it, a sharp reader guesses the ending.

**What is actually running**

The Horned God is not paid a weekly stipend. He is given a king-body --- the whole store starting to inhabit him, metered by the geis so it does not warp him to death in a week. Year 7 would have been a controlled release into Danu: a ripe king bursting into the Green hoard.

Morrígan's real contract, already taught in the prologue: she spends champions when the campaign requires it. She has never been a calendar. She did not promise him year 7. She promised him a meaningful death.

**Three heresies, one office**

- The Shadow King / Slough Feg refused year 7 and fled into undeath. He stole time. He tried to make the term infinite.

- A loyal king would have lain down on the named date and fed her.

- This king does the third thing. He defects mid-term. He refuses the death she tries to collect. He spends the unused years against the lender.

**How the early death works**

Sequence, not simultaneous:

- He serves Danu for a stretch after the pledge --- months to about eighteen months. He still counts toward year 7. He is her weapon. The lovers still believe in year six.

- Before the root-kill, HIS lovers see the tree. He tells them, or the truth leaks. They know the kill will take them. They choose it. Willing and afraid, not serene. Last hours drop nest-grammar (mine, stay, keep) and pick up time-grammar (tonight, morning, go) without quoting the Morrígan. If there is sex it is farewell, not claiming. Do not sexualise their dying.

- He kills the root as her champion. They help. They die. The rest of the line dies without a choice. Two rooms, same hour: close-shot grief, wide-shot horror.

- THE CENTER. Sit in the grief. A bit of grief-porn is wanted here --- rooms, hands, bodies, cheap lager, the year-six object sitting on a table, the train they will not take. He is not noble. He is sick. He asked. They said yes. He survived. Narration does not call it necessary in the hour they die. In the hour it is just loss. Guilt stays specific once he can see: I put a real sacrifice on an incomplete altar.

- Post-traumatic growth happens inside the grief, not after a chapter labelled Recovery. He stops bargaining for more time. He can look at a death he caused and not rename it. He can love the dead without trying to keep them --- Danu will offer to make their deaths feed the Green; refusing that comfort is the start of defection. The strength is capacity, not hardness. He is not colder. He is able.

- The grief is what shows him the hoard, and why the second killing has to happen. If he stops at the root, he spent them for Danu. Their sacrifice must vent both ways.

- He defects. Apostasy breaks the meter. The unripened store is still in him. The body starts coming due. Short window --- days, not months.

- Danu smells the defection and tries to collect on her terms: a forced sacred-king death that would still feed her. She is not trying to murder him early. She is trying to keep him until the harvest date, and if she cannot, to harvest him herself. Two deaths are available.

- Last night: Morrígan. This is the briefing and the slow-burn payoff. He did not know he could dump the term until she shows him. Hours, not months, after he understands. He also finally has the eyes for what she has been the entire time. The last fuck is treaty, love, and instruction in how to aim a bursting vessel. She still does not say stay.

- He refuses Danu's death and spends the unused years the other way. The last warp-spasm is unlived time tearing through the Horned God-body --- antlers, heat, the years he will not have. Ugly before it is glorious. Bodily, not a mana bar. Then he is empty and he dies.

Conservation holds. He is not getting free power. He is detonating the loan. Cost is the unlived years plus the body. That is how a mid-term king kills a goddess.

**Lived time of the novel**

**LOCK.** About three to four years from the dream to the death. The deal around year two of the book. Death twelve to twenty-four months into the term. Pre-deal years (powerless / subservient / equal, the nest, the loves) do not shrink --- those loves have to be real before the cascade. After the pledge, accelerate. He expects a reign. He gets a war.

**Where the deal is struck**

A real threshold site. Strong candidates:

- Newgrange / Brú na Bóinne at winter solstice --- womb of the Earth Goddess, tourist buses by day, the opposite at night.

- The Hill of Tara --- kingship site, almost too on-the-nose, which can be used or avoided.

- Oweynagat, Rathcroghan, Co. Roscommon --- the Morrígan's cave, a gate. Best if the deal is less clean than he thinks.

- A lesser-known British overlay: Glastonbury Tor, Avebury, a locked estate in Wiltshire, a multi-storey car park in Birmingham that should not have a standing stone in the basement.

**The body of the god**

Warp-spasm is the Horned God beginning to inhabit him. Early: a shoulder that will not sit in its socket, heat coming off the skin, an eye that will not track. Mid: antler-buds under the scalp he can hide with a hood. Late: he does not fit in cars; CCTV does not know what it filmed; animals kneel or bolt. Final form is what dies, and it dies early, still unripe. Decide, before drafting, the exact line where the body-horror stops being titillating and starts being the cost. That line is the book's tone-signature.

**Peek, then hide**

The author was explicit: a lot of the Sláine material stays hidden at the outside, with a peek. The prologue is the peek. Then the book should read as modern vampire / urban-fantasy smut for a long stretch. Earth-goddess lore leaks through dreams, a standing stone, a witch who talks too much, a shifter who will not go near certain hills. The deal is the first time the Horned God is named in the present-tense narrative.
