## Voice — how the page actually reads

The desk-card cadence is not the book.

Do not write a chapter as a stack of isolated facts:

> The map. The dark glass. That is the first wrong thing. That is the crime.

That is a beat sheet. It photographs as AI even when the architecture is right. The reader's ear wants a scene that *continues*.

Write the way a professional novel carries a room: paragraphs that run, a thought that rides through an action, sentences of mixed length whose default is a full sentence with someone doing something. The next sentence picks up from the last instead of starting a new poster.

Model for Book 1 sex-and-power rooms: C. S. Pacat (*Captive Prince*) more than Hemingway posters or Carey ornament. Controlled close third. Dry talk. Body and mind in the same sentence. No thesis after the fact.

**Gold sample:** Chapter 16 (Study) as dropped 2026-09-17, the continuous-prose pass. Steal that engine for 13, 17—18, 23, 29—30, 34 when those rooms get a quality run. Do not steal 16's *content* into those rooms.

00—02 stay as published. Do not polish them in this pass. 03 is the witch-category patch. 09 is not to be continued as prose.

## Do

- Present tense. Close third. Manchester in the mouth.
- Let a paragraph hold more than one beat if the beats belong together.
- Put interiority *inside* the action (*he watches his own fingers undo the buttons*) instead of labelling the moral afterwards (*that is the crime*).
- Vary sentence length. A short line is for impact after a long one, not the default.
- Name objects once, then use them. Clothes-line, chair, map, tea.
- Dialogue can be dry and unfinished. People in this house do not explain the theme out loud unless they are Cairn doing a job.
- Sex is specific and physical. Power is who holds the end. Stay in the mixed state when the room requires it; do not tidy.

## Do not

- Three-word sentence stacks as a house style (*The map. The glass. The rope.*). One fragment is fine. A page of them is not.
- *That is the X.* / *Both true.* / *He files it.* / *The yet.* as a metronome. Once in a book, maybe. Not every exit.
- Crow-on-the-hedge as a chapter button. Weather can be weather.
- *The unnamed man* on the page.
- *BIND* / *ENTER* / *KEEP* dumped as all-caps lecture if the body already showed the thing. The verb can stay where the house uses it; the reader should feel it before they get the label.
- Merge rooms to get a prettier paragraph. 16 is the chair and the hand. Not Joss. Not Priya's tile. Not the blood.
- Past tense in Book 1 proper (prologue excepted).
- Winking the blood will fail, naming Morrígan as source, year-six numbers before 34.
