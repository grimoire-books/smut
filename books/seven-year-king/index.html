He does not look at the crow. He says, "Aye."

Priya, because Priya builds: "There's a cottage. A winter. A cooker. He's coming. Joss is coming. Drew knows now too. We're planning it. That's a fact."

The woman at the stone listens as if a deposit were a hymn.

"Plan it," she says. "A place. A person. A winter you think you will still have. Year six. Speak it. That is how a term is believed."

Year six. The number lands. He hears himself doing it.

"Year six," he says. "The cottage. Priya. Joss if he puts the toast back. Drew told. A week that is a winter. I will still have it."

Priya makes a sound that is not a word. Joss says nothing, which is worse.

The woman puts her hand on his chest, over the jumper, over the heart that has been listening since the dream.

"Husband," she says. "The land takes a king that way. You said the years. You said the winter. Now you say the rest with your body."

She takes him on the wet ground and does not pretend it is a wedding.

Not the hall. Not the tile. Not the sheet. Harvest. She draws the jumper off him in the wind and the cold finds the burn-line and does not care. Her mouth is warm. She kisses him like a contract being signed. Teeth, then not. She puts him down on grit and moss and gets over him with her dress already dealt with, and looks at him while she takes him in.

"Seven," she says.

"Seven."

"Then the earth."

"Then the earth."

She fucks him like a season arriving. No rope. No knife. The leak opens and the land comes up through the stone and through her and through him and he stays in the room because if he goes out now he will take the hill and the city and the shelf. She does not drink him the house way. She takes what the vow takes. Heat. Copper. Crushed green. His bad shoulder sits and then does not sit and then sits again.

Priya is on the road. He can hear her not coming closer. Joss is a stillness that has decided not to be a knife tonight.

"Look at me," the woman says.

He looks. She is beautiful in a way that does not ask him to like it. She is not the ford. She is a marriage. He comes with her mouth on his throat and the date already spoken. She comes after, or she does not; the land does something that uses her face. She does not say stay. She does not say mine. She says husband, once, as if the word were a harvest.

A crow in the thorn after. Silent. He does not look at it when he names the date, and he does not look at it after.

She stands. She is dressed. The stone is only a stone if you are a coward.

"Go home," she says. "Wear the years. Do the work. I will find you when the land needs a mouth."

He walks toward the land. The house is behind him. Priya and Joss on the road. Drew in a doorway he cannot see. Cairn somewhere on the mile, not blessing it.

Priya takes his arm. Not a leash. A person. Joss walks on the other side and does not spit.

"Seven years," Priya says.

"Aye."

"We're still building the week."

"Aye."

The clock has started.

He believes the seven years.

That is the whole night.
