# Chapter 11
## Ours

The damp house still takes rent.

He goes back once in the week because the flatmate texts a shopping list with his name not on it, which is a way of asking. The carpet is still damp. The mug with the chip is still on the draining board. He washes it and leaves it.

"You living there now," the flatmate says.

"Visit."

"Right."

It is not a fight. It is a fact that has not found its sentence. The protein tub has a skin on it. He does not scrape it. He takes the charger out of his bag, looks at it, and puts it back. Pride. Fire exit. He cannot tell which.

On the bus to Palatine he thinks about not getting off. The thought is small and stupid and it hurts in the same place as the shoulder. Leaving would mean the key on the table and Priya's radio and Drew's stay and Joss on the stairs and Cairn's coat that does not steam.

Leaving would hurt.

He gets off.

The door opens before he knocks. Priya does not make a speech about it. There is pasta. Joss is in a towel and does not touch him. Drew puts a plate in front of him as if the plate were an argument he has already won.

Cairn, from the study door: "You went to the other house."

"Rent."

"Don't make a habit of proving you can. The smell is still on you. Other noses will find the damp if you keep going back to sleep in it."

He eats. The pasta is good. The radio is doing something Irish and old that is not a hymn. Joss steals a piece of toast off his plate as a smaller war, and Priya, without looking up, says, "Put it back," and Joss puts it back, and the table holds.

---

Later Joss kisses him in the kitchen on a night that has been almost civil.

Not asked, not quite the hall. A grab, a mouth, teeth, the taste of the dark stuff Cairn drinks. The unnamed man could wreck him. He kisses back for three seconds because the body votes and then he puts Joss off with a hand on his chest.

"Not tonight."

Joss's eyes, pleased and furious. "Please. Please can I take you upstairs. You know the word. You used it on me at the sink. I stopped. I will stop."

"Not tonight is not the word. Not tonight is not tonight."

Joss laughs, sharp, and for a second is a person who got taken badly, the weather report, and then he is Joss again. "Boring. Fine. Drew can keep you. Priya can feed you. I'll be here when you want the thing you wanted on your knees."

He steals nothing this time. He goes upstairs. Music goes on and comes off. The unnamed man stays at the table with a plate and a pulse and does not follow.

---

Cairn finds him in the back room with the cracked phone and the BBC paragraphs still open, the Sperrins one, which he has not closed in days. Cairn reads over his shoulder without asking. The unnamed man does not hide it. Keeping it is not the same as hiding.

"Don't," Cairn says.

"I'm not doing anything."

"You're sniffing a grave that isn't yours." Cairn's voice is the dock. "Rural messes happen. They are not all our messes. Some belong to the other flag. Some belong to people who wash things in rivers. You don't need them. You need to last. Close it."

He closes it. The seed sits. Cairn does not ask what he dreamed. Cairn never has, not since the dock. The unnamed man almost wants to be asked, which is new, and does not speak, which is old.

"You're ours," Cairn says, at the door, not looking back. "That is not a romance. It is a fact I am willing to defend. Don't make it expensive."

Ours. Priya's word, Cairn's word, Drew's mine, Joss's marriage joke. The unnamed man sits on the bed that is a bed now and feels the house around him: four other hearts, if they have hearts, the hedge, the leak pointed at a candle-sized life. He is not named. He is not nothing. He could walk. The walking would hurt. That is the thing that was missing when the key was only a key.

He goes downstairs. Priya has the radio on. Drew has his feet on Joss, who allows it as a truce. Cairn in the doorway of the study, the map, the war. The unnamed man sits on the floor with his back to the sofa because there is no chair left, and nobody tells him to get up, and Joss's fingers find his hair, brief, not a grab, and he does not say the word, and he does not wreck anyone, and Priya puts a plate on his knee without asking if he is hungry.

Drew talks about a boy in Ancoats who said thank you. Priya tells him to shut up. Joss, almost idle, says, "The hole sat in a mess tonight and didn't eat the girl. Growth." It is almost a compliment. It is also a reminder that Joss can smell what happened. The unnamed man eats so he does not have to answer. Cairn, from the study door, says, "Don't call him the hole in my hearing," and Joss says, "What do I call him then," and nobody has a name, and the radio fills it, and that is allowed.

He is hungry. He eats. The house is a house. Outside, the crow on the hedge is only a crow, and he does not go to the window to check, because he does not need to, and that, too, is a kind of staying.

Mark, in the morning, says, "You're somewhere else."

"Aye."

"Don't drop the kettles."

He does not. The warehouse kettles are kettles. He will, one day, drop the job instead. Not yet. Yet is still doing a lot of work. The warehouse smells like cardboard. Palatine smells like polish and old blood and pasta. He knows which one he will walk toward at four. He knows it the way he knew, on a bus, that he could take a bag and would not. The would-not is changing shape. He files that. He does not tell Cairn. He picks. He packs. He goes home, and the word is not a lie, not entirely, not today.

At the damp house that night he does not sleep. He puts the Vale key on the windowsill next to the cracked phone and looks at both of them until they are only metal. Then he puts the Vale key back in his pocket, leaves the phone, leaves the chipped mug, and walks Palatine in the rain. The door opens before he knocks. Priya does not ask where he has been. She puts tea down. Joss is civil in a shirt. Drew says *stay* as if the night were a room he had already paid for.

He stays.

The rented house is still a house.

It is not this one.
