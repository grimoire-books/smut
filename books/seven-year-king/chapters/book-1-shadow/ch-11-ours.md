# Chapter 11
## Ours

The damp house still takes rent.

He goes back once in the week because the flatmate texts a shopping list with his name not on it, which is a way of asking. The carpet is still damp. The mug with the chip is still on the draining board. He washes it and leaves it.

"You living there now," the flatmate says.

"Visit."

"Right."

It is not a fight. It is a fact that has not found its sentence.

On the bus to Palatine he thinks about not getting off. The thought is small and stupid and it hurts in the same place as the shoulder. Leaving would mean the key on the table and Priya's radio and Drew's stay and Joss in the hall and Cairn's coat that does not steam.

Leaving would hurt.

He gets off.

The door opens before he knocks. Priya does not make a speech about it. There is pasta. Joss is in a towel and does not touch him. Drew puts a plate in front of him as if the plate were an argument he has already won.

Cairn, from the study door: "You went to the other house."

"Rent."

"Don't make a habit of proving you can. The smell is still on you. Other noses will find the damp if you keep going back to sleep in it."

He eats.

Later, in the back room, he looks at the charger still in the bag and does not take it out. That is not a plan. That is pride. He knows the difference and it does not help.

He sleeps here.

The rented house is still a house.

It is not this one.
