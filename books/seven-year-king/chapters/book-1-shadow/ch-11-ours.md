# Chapter 11
## Ours

Weeks, not a montage: a Thursday, another Thursday, a payday he does not go to the club. Sian does not appear. He does not look. The leak learns the size of a candle, a room, a house. Sometimes a street. Never, if he is trying, a tram.

He pays the flatmate two months because Priya puts cash in his hand and says it is not a gift it is the house not being a thief, and the flatmate says, "Jesus, what job," and he says, "Private," which is a lie that holds. He leaves the chip-mug. He takes the charger. The damp house becomes a place he could go, and then a place he does not.

Joss kisses him in the kitchen on a night that has been almost civil.

Not asked, not quite forced. A grab, a mouth, teeth, the taste of the dark stuff Cairn drinks. The unnamed man could wreck him. He kisses back for three seconds because the body votes and then he puts Joss off with a hand on his chest and says, "Ask."

Joss's eyes, pleased and furious. "Please," he says, like a swear. "Please can I kiss you. Please can I take you upstairs and have you say kettles when you mean it, not as a personality."

"Not tonight."

"Kettles."

"No. Not tonight is not kettles. Not tonight is not tonight."

Joss laughs, sharp, and for a second is a person who got taken badly, the weather report, and then he is Joss again. "Boring. Fine. Drew can keep you. Priya can feed you. I'll be here when you want the thing you wanted on your knees." He steals a piece of toast off the unnamed man's plate as a smaller war, and Priya, without looking up, says, "Put it back," and Joss puts it back, and the table holds.

---

Cairn finds him in the back room with the cracked phone and the BBC paragraphs still open, the Sperrins one, which he has not closed in days. Cairn reads over his shoulder without asking. The unnamed man does not hide it. Keeping it is not the same as hiding.

"Don't," Cairn says.

"I'm not doing anything."

"You're sniffing a grave that isn't yours." Cairn's voice is the dock. "Rural messes happen. They are not all our messes. Some belong to the other flag. Some belong to people who wash things in rivers. You don't need them. You need to last. Close it."

He closes it. The seed sits. Cairn does not ask what he dreamed. Cairn never has, not since the dock. The unnamed man almost wants to be asked, which is new, and does not speak, which is old.

"You're ours," Cairn says, at the door, not looking back. "That is not a romance. It is a fact I am willing to defend. Don't make it expensive."

Ours. Priya's word, Cairn's word, Drew's mine, Joss's marriage joke. The unnamed man sits on the bed that is a bed now and feels the house around him: four other hearts, if they have hearts, the hedge, the leak pointed at a candle-sized life. He is not named. He is not nothing. He could walk. The walking would hurt. That is the thing that was missing when the key was only a key.

He goes downstairs. Priya has the radio on. Drew has his feet on Joss, who allows it as a truce. Cairn in the doorway of the study, the map, the war. The unnamed man sits on the floor with his back to the sofa because there is no chair left, and nobody tells him to get up, and Joss's fingers find his hair, brief, not a grab, and he does not say kettles, and he does not wreck anyone, and Priya puts a plate on his knee without asking if he is hungry.

Drew talks about a boy in Ancoats who said thank you. Priya tells him to shut up. Joss, almost idle, says, "The hole sat in a mess tonight and didn't eat the girl. Growth." It is almost a compliment. It is also a reminder that Joss can smell what happened. The unnamed man eats so he does not have to answer. Cairn, from the study door, says, "Don't call him the hole in my hearing," and Joss says, "What do I call him then," and nobody has a name, and the radio fills it, and that is allowed.

He is hungry. He eats. The house is a house. Outside, the crow on the hedge is only a crow, and he does not go to the window to check, because he does not need to, and that, too, is a kind of staying.

Mark, in the morning, says, "You're somewhere else."

"Aye."

"Don't drop the kettles."

He does not. He will, one day, drop the job instead. Not yet. Yet is still doing a lot of work. The warehouse smells like cardboard. Palatine smells like polish and old blood and pasta. He knows which one he will walk toward at four. He knows it the way he knew, on a bus, that he could take a bag and would not. The would-not is changing shape. He files that. He does not tell Cairn. He picks. He packs. He goes home, and the word is not a lie, not entirely, not today.
