# Chapter 15
## Standing

Saturday. Cairn names the pack the way he names a street.

"Land-pack. Not a house like this. Stones. Stories. They think they are the clean side. They are not clean. They keep what they keep. Ruth is the one who will talk. The rest will not. You do not go in."

They walk past the pub.

It is a pub. Boarded sign, new paint, a dog tied to a ring. Music that is only music. He could go in. His hand knows the door the way it knew the girl on the sofa.

He does not go in.

A lad on the step looks at him and smiles as if they have already met. Pack-boy. The smile is not kind and not unkind. Cairn does not stop walking.

"Table is home," Cairn says, after. "You will be useful there. You will not be useful in that room until I say. If you walk in on your own I will not come and get you in a taxi with a kind story."

The unnamed man files the smile next to the warm stone next to milk at a back door. He does not make a religion of it. He also does not pretend he did not want to go in. Wanting is not kneeling. Cairn, for once, does not ask him to pretend.

---

The damp house gets a text that is not a shopping list.

Keep the deposit. Someone else is moving in Monday. Leave the key under the pot that isn't a pot.

He goes once more. The carpet is still damp. The chipped mug is still on the draining board. He washes it and leaves it. He takes nothing else. The protein tub can be someone else's dare. The flatmate is not in. That is a mercy. He puts the rented key under the pot that is not a pot and walks away from a house that was never a house, only a room that took rent.

On Palatine the door opens before he knocks. Priya looks at his empty hands.

"Good," she says. "I was going to send Joss, and Joss would have made a speech."

He puts the Vale key on the hook by the coats. It hangs. It does not bite. That is as much funeral as the damp is going to get.

---

Ruth is on the corner with the tote. She does not talk too much. She looks at his face and then at Cairn, who has not come out.

"Pack's at the park," she says. "Pint if you want one that isn't a conversion. Land isn't a mother who never slapped. Remember that when they smile."

"I'm not going in."

"I know. He told you not to." She tips her head. "Stones then stories then a pint then a kneeling then a name. That's their staircase. Walking past is allowed. Kneeling is how they get you. You already know about kneeling."

He almost asks whose name. He does not. A name is armour until the house claims one, and the house has not, and he is not going to pick one off a pub step.

"The stone's still warm," she says. "That's not always a kindness. Tell him I said."

She goes. The tote knocks her knee. The unnamed man walks home. Home is the table. The table is pasta and Joss being civil and Drew's knee against his under the wood.

Priya: "You went for a walk."

"Aye."

"Eat."

He eats. The pub stays a pub he did not enter. The smile stays on the step in his head. The deposit is gone. The chipped mug is someone else's. The charger is on the sill in the back room. The canal stone is warm.

Joss, almost idle: "You look like a person who lives here."

"Don't."

"I'm allowed to notice. I live on a street."

Cairn, from the study door: "He lives here. That is not a romance. It is a fact. Eat your pasta."

The unnamed man eats his pasta. The radio fills the place where a name would go. Outside, the crow on the hedge is only a crow. He does not go to the window. He does not go to the pub. He sits at the table until sitting is the thing he knows how to do, and that, tonight, is standing.
