# Chapter 15
## Standing

The days after the ask are a different temperature, not a new house.

Joss does not become kind. Kind would be a conversion trick of his own. He is less bored. He steals toast and puts it back before Priya speaks. He does not spit. He finds the unnamed man in the back room once and kisses him like a question, and the unnamed man says aye, and it is only a kiss, and Joss leaves before it becomes a belt, which is manners, which is new.

Drew is sulky for a day and then not, because Drew does not hold sulk when there are other mouths in the city. He says *mine* about a Tuesday and the unnamed man says *we'll see* and Drew laughs, and the KEEP is still there, under the laugh, a seed. Priya feeds everyone as if a siege has been upgraded to an occupation. Cairn is on the phone more. The map acquires another mark, then another. The unnamed man does not ask. He is useful. He stands behind. He does not pull.

He quits being a guest in his own head.

That is smaller than it sounds. It is putting the charger by the bed. It is a second mug, not the chip one. It is telling the flatmate, by text, *keep the rest of the deposit I'm not coming back*, and the flatmate sending a thumbs-up and *told you*, and that being the whole funeral of the damp. It is walking Palatine without counting the stops to the warehouse. It is sleeping in Joss's bed once more, not asked as a scene, asked as a night, kettles unused, Joss's arm careful, morning toast.

Cairn takes a little, asked, in the study, twice in a week. The unnamed man says aye both times. He is not on his knees. He is not tied. He could say no. He does not. That is subservient without the hall: the war in Cairn's mouth, the map, useful. After the second time he is shaky and Priya sits with him on the back-room bed and talks about nothing, a bus route, a woman at the shops, until his hands work, and that is aftercare too, and he files it next to Joss's clumsy cloth.

---

Ruth is on Palatine with the tote.

Not at the stone. At the corner, as if corners were neutral. The unnamed man has a bag of shopping. She has no milk this time. She looks tired. She talks too much before he has said hello.

"Don't panic. I'm not converting you in the street. I wanted you to know the impolite one isn't me. There's a pack on the edge of the park that won't come in because of Vale. Land-side. They felt the stone too. I told them you were a person, not a door. I may have lied a bit. You might be a door. I'm nervous. I talk."

"Cairn will hate this."

"Cairn hates weather." She glances at the hedge. The crow is there. She glances at the crow longer than a woman should, then away, as if she has manners about birds. "If you ever want the other weather report, there's a pub on the old crossroads past the park. Not a trap. A pint. I will talk too much and you can leave. Bring your house if you must. Don't bring the pretty cruel one, he smells like a knife and the pack will start something."

"I'm ours."

"I know. That's why I'm being polite." She almost puts a hand on his arm and does not. "The land isn't a mother who never slapped anyone. Your man is right about that, even when he's wrong about everything else. Remember I said it. You'll need it later and you won't want it from me then."

She goes. He takes the shopping in. He tells Cairn, because not telling is how you get converted by accident.

Cairn swears, once, which is rare, and then laughs, once, which is rarer. "A pint. A pack. A woman who leaves milk. That's a recruitment board. You will not go to the pub."

"I wasn't going to."

"You were going to think about it. That's the first pull. Stones, then stories, then a pint, then a kneeling, then a name you didn't choose." Cairn's dock-look. "You have a name you haven't chosen yet either. Don't let them give you one first. When a name arrives in this house it will be a claim we all hear. Until then you are ours without a label. It is inconvenient. It is also armour."

Priya, in the door: "Let him walk past the pub if he wants. Walking past is not kneeling. You said that about the stone."

Cairn looks at her. The war, unnamed, sits between them. Then he says, "Walking past. Not in. If a pack-boy smiles at you, you come home. If a stone is warm, you come home. If anyone says goddess like a gift, you come home. Home is this table."

"Aye," the unnamed man says, and means the table, and the meaning is a leash and a fact and a plate, and he does not sort those, not tonight.

Joss, later, in the back room door: "If you go to a witch-pub without me I will be offended. That's not an ask. That's weather."

"I'm not going."

"Good nightmare." Joss's mouth, brief, the good shoulder. "Sleep. Tomorrow Cairn will make you useful and I will steal toast and the stone will be warm without you, which is how stones ought to behave."

He sleeps. He dreams, not the ford, not the platform: a table, a stone, a pint he does not drink, a crow on a hedge, antlers in a fog that does not get to be a picture. He wakes. The dream is already going. He does not write it. He does not tell Cairn. He puts his hand on the mattress, which is only a mattress, and hears the house: Priya's radio, Drew's laugh, Joss's pipe, Cairn's study, four temperatures, one roof.

He is still unnamed. He is still a leak. He is still a man who could wreck them and has not. The house would hurt to leave. The stone is warm without him. The crow is at the edge.

That is the whole news, for now. It is not nothing. It is not a king-story. It is a Thursday, and toast, and a stop that worked, and a woman with milk, and a war on a map, and a seed that has started to dream kitchens as well as rivers, which is how they get you, which is also, this morning, the reason he gets up.
