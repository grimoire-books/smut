# Chapter 14
## The Stone

He goes back to the churchyard because Cairn said walk past, and walking past a thing is how you make it ordinary, and the stone has not become ordinary.

Daylight. Palatine at two, a nothing hour. The leak is candle-sized. The stone is still slightly too warm. He does not kneel. He stands with his hands in his pockets like a man waiting for a bus that does not stop here. A crow on the wall. Of course a crow. He does not nod.

"You can hear it, then," a woman says.

Not the platform woman. Younger. A good coat that has been in weather. A tote with books that have seen better decades. She has been leaving milk, he thinks, and then he sees she has a carton in the tote, unopened, which is worse, because it means he is guessing religions in churchyards like a fool.

"I can hear a stone," he says. "That's not a sentence I say."

"It is now." She looks at his face, his wrists, the rumour of Joss, and does not comment. "I'm Ruth. I do a bit of work at the university that isn't the work they think I do. You're Vale's. Don't look like that, the house-smell is a smell. I'm not here to steal you. I'm here because that stone has been quiet for ten years and this week it isn't, and you keep walking past it like a dog that won't piss."

"Cairn said don't kneel."

"Cairn would." She is not unkind. She talks too much; he can hear the extra sentences queuing. "They're hungry people. They think the ground is a story the other side tells to feel clean. Sometimes they're right. Sometimes the ground is just the ground, and it likes who it likes, and it liked you before Vale put a mug in your hand. That's not a recruitment. That's a weather report."

The Earth Goddess is not a name in her mouth. The land is. He files the difference. He does not fall in love with a simplicity. He also does not walk away as fast as Cairn would like.

"What do you want."

"To see if you'd put your hand on it if someone asked." She shrugs. "Don't. Your man is right about kneeling. I'm asking you to notice. When the stones notice someone, the land will send more than me. I'm the polite version. The impolite version has teeth. Or antlers. I'm not being poetic. I talk too much when I'm nervous. You're making the air smell like a storm and a kitchen."

"I have to go."

"Go. Tell Vale I said the stone is awake and I didn't touch his boy. He'll still hate me. That's the job." She puts the milk down at the back of the churchyard wall, not a joke, a habit, and sees him see it. "Don't make a religion. It's milk. The things that like milk have been here longer than Palatine Road."

He walks. He does not put his hand on the stone. The crow hops once, which is not a nod. At the hedge he looks back and Ruth is gone, or is a woman with books, which is allowed.

---

Cairn already knows. Of course.

"I said notice, don't invite," Cairn says, in the study, the map, a new mark that might be the churchyard. "You stood there long enough to be a conversation."

"She stood. I stood. I didn't kneel."

"Names."

"Ruth. University. Milk. She said the stone liked me before the mug."

Cairn's face does the tired thing. "They always say before. It's a conversion trick. You were a leak in a warehouse. We found you. They will say the land found you first. Both can be true. Only one of those truths keeps you from bleeding trams." He pours the dark stuff, does not offer. "You will see more of them. Standing stones, women with milk, men who talk about the Goddess as if she were a mother who has never slapped anyone. Do not be rude. Do not be converted. You are ours. That is still not a romance. It is a fact I will defend against a stone."

"She said the impolite version has antlers."

Cairn goes still in a way Joss went still on the stairs. Then he drinks. "Did she."

"Aye."

"File it. Don't hunt it. Antlers are a story they tell about kings. We have enough king-stories in this house and they all end badly." Cairn looks at him, dock-look. "Joss. Last night. Are you intact."

"Aye."

"Is he."

"Aye."

"Good. I need both of you standing. The other flag is moving milk around my postcode and a stone has woken up because you have a leak. That is not a coincidence I enjoy. Candle at nine. Smaller. If you can feel the stone from this room we have a problem."

He sits. He feels the candle. He feels the house. He does not feel the stone, not from here, and Cairn's mouth does a thing that is almost relief. Priya puts her head in with tea for the unnamed man only, a snub Cairn accepts as tax. Joss, passing, says, "You smell like a churchyard," and it is not quite an insult, and Drew says, "Don't bring a witch home, they don't like the curtains," and Priya says, "You don't like the curtains," and the table, later, holds, and the unnamed man eats, and the milk at the wall is a fact in the city, and he does not make a religion, and the seed sits up when he thinks *antlers* and he puts it down again, because Cairn said file, don't hunt, and because the house is pasta and a stop that worked and a back room, and the stone is a warmth he walked past, and walking past is still the job.

At the window, after, the crow is on the hedge. He almost tells it about Ruth. He does not. He says, not tonight, not even a whisper, only a thought, and the crow, who is only a crow, or is not, stays at the edge, which is all it is allowed, yet, and the yet is getting a different shape, and he files that too, and goes to bed in a house that would hurt to leave, which is the point of a house, which is how they get you, which is also, tonight, fine.
