# Chapter 17
## The Back Room

Priya finds him because the house is small and because he has not come down for tea, which is a religion he has not missed before.

She does not knock the way a guest knocks. She sees the clothes. She sees the wrist. She sees his face. She does not ask *what happened* first. She puts a glass of water on the floor where he can reach it without being handed it like a child. She sits on the boards, not the bed, until he looks at her.

"Cairn," she says. Not a question.

"Aye."

"Did you ask."

"No."

"Did you say the word."

"Aye. It wasn't for him."

Priya's face does the small grief, the one that would not photograph, and then it does something harder. "I'll kill him."

"Don't." He means it. He does not know why he means it. "Then I have to leave. I don't— the table." He hates that sentence. He says it anyway. "I still live here. He said that. It's true. That's the—" He cannot say leash. "That's the thing."

She does not lecture. She does not say *true love lets go*. She says, "Drink the water," and he drinks the water, and his hands shake, and she does not steady them for him. After a while she says, "Can I sit on the bed."

"Aye."

She sits. Not touching. The radio downstairs is still a radio. Drew laughs, far, and the laugh goes through the unnamed man like a nail. Priya says, without looking at the door, "I'll tell him to shut up," and he says, "Don't," because a house that goes quiet for him is a house that has made this a funeral, and it is not a funeral, it is a Thursday, it is a study, it is a fact.

Joss is in the doorway. Of course Joss. He takes in the wrist, the water, Priya on the bed, and his face is the blade without the sport.

"I'll kill him," Joss says, which is becoming a chorus.

"She said that."

"She doesn't mean it like I mean it." Joss's hands are fists. "He used the rope. I can smell it. I stopped. He didn't. That's not a house. That's a—"

"It's the house," the unnamed man says, and the saying is a flinch. "Go away. Both of you. I need—" He does not have the word. "A minute that isn't a meeting."

Priya goes. Joss does not, not at once. Joss says, "I fetched water. Don't tell anyone." It is almost a joke. It dies. He puts the glass closer with his foot, which is the clumsiness of the cloth, and then he goes, and the unnamed man hears him on the stairs, not to the study, to his own room, and a door that wants to be heard.

He showers. The door shuts. He watches spend and river-iron-not-river go down the plughole and does not feel clean. He cannot bear his own cock. He cannot bear that it worked. He puts clothes on like armour that does not fit. He sits on the bed. He does not sleep. Cairn's footprint in his head fades to a smear, not gone. The stone is quiet. That, at least, is the door Cairn meant. He hates that it worked too.

In the night he flinches when a pipe knocks. He flinches when he puts his own hand on his own wrist. He does not flinch at the crow, which is on the hedge, which is weather. He almost wants the crow to be a person so he could have a sentence that is not this house. He does not go to the window. He lies on his back and looks at the ceiling and does not tidy anything into a lesson.

He thinks, because the mind will not stop, of Joss saying *I stopped* in the dark, proud and furious. He thinks of Priya's cloth in the kitchen, the first night of spit. He thinks of Sian's nosebleed and a fig candle. None of it is this. This is a separate room. He will not let the rooms collapse into one bed, one word, one *they*. Joss is Joss. Cairn is the dock. The table is still a table. He holds the distinctions because if he does not, he will have to leave, and leaving would hurt, and the hurt is the leash, and he is not ready to cut it. He hates that he is not ready. He files the hate next to the graph he has not drawn yet.

Toward dawn he sleeps, if you can call it that. He does not dream the ford. He dreams the chair. He wakes with an erection and hate, both, and waits both down, and does not make either of them a vote. The crow is there at first light. He does not nod. He gets up because getting up is the sentence he still knows how to say.
