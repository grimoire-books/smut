# Chapter 26
## Past

Ruth is on Palatine with the tote. Not at the stone. At the corner, as if corners were neutral. The unnamed man has a bag of shopping. She has no milk this time. She looks tired. She talks too much before he has said hello.

"Don't panic. I'm not converting you in the street. I wanted you to know the impolite one isn't me. There's a pack on the edge of the park that won't come in because of Vale. Land-side. They felt the stone too. I told them you were a person, not a door. I may have lied a bit. You might be a door. I'm nervous. I talk."

"Cairn will hate this."

"Cairn hates weather." She glances at the hedge. The crow is there. She glances at the crow longer than a woman should, then away. "If you ever want the other weather report, there's a pub on the old crossroads past the park. Not a trap. A pint. I will talk too much and you can leave. Bring your house if you must. Don't bring the pretty cruel one, he smells like a knife and the pack will start something."

"I'm ours."

"I know. That's why I'm being polite." She almost puts a hand on his arm and does not. "The land isn't a mother who never slapped anyone. Your man is right about that, even when he's wrong about everything else. Remember I said it. You'll need it later and you won't want it from me then."

She goes. He takes the shopping in. He tells Cairn, because not telling is how you get converted by accident.

Cairn swears, once, which is rare, and then laughs, once, which is rarer. "A pint. A pack. A woman who leaves milk. That's a recruitment board. You will not go to the pub."

"I wasn't going to."

"You were going to think about it. That's the first pull. Stones, then stories, then a pint, then a kneeling, then a name you didn't choose." Cairn's dock-look. "You have a name you haven't chosen yet either. Don't let them give you one first."

Priya, in the door: "Let him walk past the pub if he wants. Walking past is not kneeling. You said that about the stone."

Cairn looks at her. Then he says, "Walking past. Not in. If a pack-boy smiles at you, you come home. If a stone is warm, you come home. If anyone says goddess like a gift, you come home. Home is this table."

"Aye," the unnamed man says, and means the table.

He walks past at dusk. The pub is a pub. A boy on the step, land-side, a smile that is not a city smile. The unnamed man does not go in. He does not smile back long enough to be a conversation. He comes home. Tells Cairn. Cairn: first pull. Table. He did not go in.

Joss, later, in the back room door: "If you go to a witch-pub without me I will be offended. That's not an ask. That's weather."

"I'm not going."

"Good nightmare." Joss's mouth, brief, the good shoulder. "Sleep. Tomorrow Cairn will make you useful and I will steal toast and the stone will be warm without you, which is how stones ought to behave."

He sleeps. He dreams, not the ford, not the platform: a table, a stone, a pint he does not drink, a crow on a hedge. He wakes. The dream is already going. He does not write it. He does not tell Cairn. He puts his hand on the mattress, which is only a mattress, and hears the house: Priya's radio, Drew's laugh, Joss's pipe, Cairn's study, four temperatures, one roof.

He is still unnamed. He is still a leak. He is still a man who could wreck them and has not. The house would hurt to leave. The stone is warm without him. The crow is at the edge. That is the whole news, for now. It is not a king-story. It is a Thursday, and toast, and a stop that worked, and a woman with milk, and a war on a map, and a seed that has started to dream kitchens as well as fords, which is how they get you, which is also, this morning, the reason he gets up.
