# Chapter 12
## Habit

The warehouse ends the way cheap things end: not with a speech. Mark says, "You're somewhere else," for the fifth time, and the unnamed man says, "Aye," and hands in the hi-vis, and Mark says, "Don't come back crying," and he does not, and the Tesco crow watches a man walk out of Trafford Park in ordinary clothes as if it has been waiting for the costume change.

Priya, when he tells her, puts a plate down harder than the plate deserves. "Good. I was going to poison that sandwich myself."

"You don't poison people."

"I poison meal deals. It's a calling." She looks at him. "You'll go mad in the house all day. Cairn will use you. That's not a warning, it's a timetable."

Cairn uses him. Days, not a montage: a candle, a street, a closed tap in a stairwell in Salford that is almost the Ancoats mess and is not, because this time the man in the kitchen looks at Cairn and nods and shuts it himself. The unnamed man stands behind. He does not pull. He gets a nosebleed once and not again. Cairn says, "Useful," which is the same as good night.

Asked taking becomes a habit, which is how BIND stops being a scene and starts being a house. Priya, wrist, kitchen, pasta after. Drew, greedier, *stay* against the cut, and the unnamed man saying *not all night* which Drew treats as flirting and which is a boundary. Cairn, once, in the study, no theatre: "I will take a little. You may say no. The war is not your problem yet. My being able to stand up is."

"Aye."

Cairn's mouth on his wrist is not a kiss. It is a tool. The unnamed man feels the war in it, unnamed, a pressure like a weather system, and then Cairn stops, and says thank you, which is worse than useful, and goes back to the map. The unnamed man sits until his hands stop shaking and then goes to the kitchen and Priya, who always knows, puts tea down and does not ask.

Joss does not ask. Joss watches the wrists the way a dog watches a plate. The unnamed man lets him watch. The yet between them has become a piece of furniture. People walk around it.

---

He learns the house in daylight.

Priya's radio. Drew's record player, which is a personality. The study door that is never quite shut. Joss's room, which he has not been in, which smells of expensive soap and something animal when the door is ajar. The photographs: mill, ship, candles, the boy. He asks Priya, once, who the boy is.

"Joss," she says. "Before. Don't tell him I said. He likes being invented."

The boy in the picture has not learned contempt yet. The unnamed man files that next to the weather report.

He walks Palatine in the afternoon like a man with no shift. The hedge. A fox in daylight, rude. A standing stone in a churchyard he has passed twenty times and not seen, because seeing it requires the leak, and the leak, pointed at candles, has started to notice ground. The stone is only a stone. It is also slightly too warm. He does not put his hand on it. A woman with a dog looks at him looking at the stone and looks away, which is Manchester manners.

He tells Cairn about the stone because the leak is Cairn's problem as well as his.

"Don't," Cairn says. "That's the other flag's furniture. You will fall in love with a simplicity. Stones are a simplicity. We are a house. Stay in the house."

"It was warm."

"They are. That's how they get you." Cairn almost smiles. "You're allowed to walk past. You're not allowed to kneel. You've done enough kneeling."

Joss, in the doorway, having heard: "He can kneel if he asks. That's the new fashion. Consent. Very modern."

"Fuck off, Joss."

"Make me." Joss looks at the unnamed man, not at Cairn. "Or ask me. I'm collecting asks. I have an empty box."

The unnamed man does not ask, that afternoon. He walks past the stone on the way to the shop for milk and does not kneel and does not tell the stone it is a simplicity. The stone does not care. The crow on the churchyard wall cares even less. He buys milk. He goes home. The word is less of a lie every time he uses it.

At the table Drew says, "Stay in tonight," and he stays, and Joss's foot finds his under the table and does not press, and Priya talks about a woman at the shops who left milk at a back door "for no reason, she said, which is always a reason," and Cairn looks up from a phone, sharp, and Priya says, "I'm allowed to notice the other flag, I live on a street," and Cairn says, "Notice. Don't invite," and the unnamed man files *milk at a back door* next to *warm stone* next to *people who wash things in rivers*, and does not make a religion of it.

Priya finds him after Cairn's thank-you with his hands still wrong. She does not ask. She takes his wrist — the one that is not a cut tonight — and leads him to the back room because the kitchen is a table and this is a body.

"Aye?" she says.

"Aye."

It is short. Asked. BIND made flesh, not a lesson. She undoes his jeans and puts her mouth on him, practical, kind, the same stop-before-she-has-to that she has with blood, and he comes with his hand in her hair and does not pull, and she swallows like it is another kind of taking she asked for, and she does not say mine, and she does not say stay, and she wipes her mouth with the back of her hand like a person.

"There," she says. "You're still here. That's the whole news."

He almost goes to Joss's door after. He does not. The ask is forming. It wants to be a sentence. He lets it sit overnight like dough, which is a Priya thought, and he hates that, and he sleeps, and the seed does not dream the ford. It dreams a kitchen. That is new. That is the house, getting in.
