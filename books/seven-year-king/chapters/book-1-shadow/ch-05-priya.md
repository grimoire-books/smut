# Chapter 5
## Priya

The key is on the table on Saturday as if it had grown there.

"If you're going to keep turning up," Priya says, "stop making me get off the sofa."

She takes him to Tesco. Milk. Bread. Tomatoes that have never seen a vine. Rain. She asks about his mam and he says not lately and she does not make a speech out of it.

"Meal deals aren't food."

He carries the bags. The house takes the bags.

In the kitchen she washes a glass that is already clean and then she looks at his wrist.

"I want that."

Not will you. Not may I. The want is the sentence.

He looks at the door. The door is there. Joss is not. Cairn's room is shut.

"Come here."

He comes.

She takes the wrist. Turns it. Thumb on the vein. She looks at him while she does it, not for permission, for flinch.

Then she bites.

Hot. Precise. She makes a small sound like someone tasting soup and finding it is not what they were told.

"Land," she says, against the skin. "Not Hunger. They were right about the park."

She drinks. He feels it go. The leak wants to follow her in. He holds it. She stops when she has had what she came for. Licks the two holes shut.

"There. That's you in the house."

She kisses him with the blood still in her mouth. Takes him upstairs because the kitchen is for cooking.

Her room is dry. Sheet that has been washed. Window onto the hedge. She undresses him like a job she is good at. Pushes him back. Gets on him.

No preamble that is a question. She sinks onto him and uses him with her hands on his chest. Tight. Ordinary. The bed knocks the wall and she does not care.

He comes first because his body has been waiting since the hall. She rides through it, finds hers, a punched-out sound. Gets off him. Looks at the mess as if it were washing-up.

Pasta after. Garlic again. Drew's guest is leaving when they come down, pale, smiling. Drew kisses the guest at the door and comes back for a plate.

Priya watches his face.

"You can be ours without being his."

It does not sound like rescue.

He walks home in the wet with the key in his pocket. The damp house does not know about the key. The mug with the chip is where he left it.

He sleeps. The wrist ticks once, like a shoulder.

He does not throw the key away.
