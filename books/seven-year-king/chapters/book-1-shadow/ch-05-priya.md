# Chapter 5
## Priya

The key is on the table on Saturday as if it had grown there.

"If you're going to keep turning up," Priya says, "stop making me get off the sofa."

She takes him to Tesco. Rain that has given up being weather and become the city. Milk. Bread. Tomatoes that have never seen a vine. A chicken that looks ashamed of itself. She puts the chicken in the trolley as if he might argue about protein.

"Meal deals aren't food."

"They're food."

"They're a dare." She looks at his face, then at the trolley. "When did you last eat something that didn't come in a triangle."

He has to think about it. That is the answer.

She asks about his mam in the cereal aisle, not looking at him, which is mercy.

"Not lately."

"Da?"

"Less."

She does not make a speech out of it. She puts Weetabix in because the house eats Weetabix and he is being added to the house like a tin.

He carries the bags. The house takes the bags. Palatine smells of wet hedge and someone else's Sunday.

In the kitchen she washes a glass that is already clean and then she looks at his wrist.

"I want that."

Not will you. Not may I. The want is the sentence.

He looks at the door. The door is there. Joss is not. Cairn's room is shut.

"Come here."

He comes.

She takes the wrist. Turns it. Thumb on the vein. She looks at him while she does it, not for permission, for flinch.

"If you need the word, use it," she says. "Kettles works on me the same as it works on him. I will stop. I am not him."

He does not say it.

Then she bites.

Hot. Precise. She makes a small sound like someone tasting soup and finding it is not what they were told.

"Land," she says, against the skin. "Not Hunger. They were right about the park."

She drinks. He feels it go. The leak wants to follow her in. He holds it. She stops when she has had what she came for. Licks the two holes shut with a nurse's neatness she has not earned from any ward.

"There. That's you in the house."

She kisses him with the blood still in her mouth. Takes him upstairs because the kitchen is for cooking.

Her room is dry. Sheet that has been washed. Window onto the hedge. A pencil on the sill from her hair. She undresses him like a job she is good at, not a ceremony. Jumper. Shirt. Belt. She pushes him back on the bed and looks at him as if taking inventory of a delivery she did order after all.

"You can say no."

"I know."

"Are you going to."

"No."

"Good."

She gets on him. No preamble that is a question. She sinks onto him in one long slide, tight, ordinary, and swears fond, and starts to move with her hands on his chest. The bed knocks the wall and she does not care.

He is inside her and the leak sits up and looks at the open place the way it looked at Sian. He holds it in the room. He does not go into her head. He gives her the angle she wants because her hip told him, not because a door opened.

He comes first because his body has been waiting since the hall. She rides through it, finds hers, a punched-out sound, forehead on his. Gets off him. Looks at the mess as if it were washing-up.

"Stay there."

He stays. She comes back with a cloth. She is not Joss. She does the cloth properly.

Pasta after. Garlic again. Radio on low. Drew's guest is leaving when they come down, pale, smiling, a coat that has been on the floor. Drew kisses the guest at the door and comes back for a plate.

Priya watches his face over the fork.

"You can be ours without being his."

It does not sound like rescue. It sounds like a fact she is willing to cook around.

He walks home in the wet with the key in his pocket. The damp house does not know about the key. The mug with the chip is where he left it.

He sleeps. The wrist ticks once, like a shoulder.

He does not throw the key away.
