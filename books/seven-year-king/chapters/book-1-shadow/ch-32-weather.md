# Chapter 32
## Weather

He goes back to the churchyard because Cairn said walk past, and walking past a thing is how you make it ordinary, and the stone has not become ordinary.

Daylight. Palatine at two. The leak is candle-sized. The stone is louder than it was. Heat. His left shoulder tries to leave. He keeps it. He does not kneel. He stands with his hands in his pockets like a man waiting for a bus that does not stop here. A crow on the wall. Of course a crow. He does not nod.

Ruth is there. Tote. Milk. She talks too much.

"You can hear it, then. It's been quiet for years and this week it isn't, and you keep walking past it like a dog that won't piss. Vale's. Don't look like that, the house-smell is a smell. I'm not here to steal you. I'm here because whatever woke in you, the land noticed. That's not a recruitment. That's a weather report. The impolite version has teeth. Or antlers. I'm not being poetic. I talk too much when I'm nervous. You smell like a storm and a kitchen."

"Cairn said don't kneel."

"Cairn would." She puts the milk down at the wall, not a joke, a habit. "Don't make a religion. It's milk. The things that like milk have been here longer than Palatine Road. If you ever want a pint I already told you where. Don't bring the knife-boy."

He walks. He does not put his hand on the stone. The crow hops once, which is not a nod. At the hedge he looks back and Ruth is gone, or is a woman with books, which is allowed.

Cairn already knows.

"I said notice, don't invite," Cairn says, in the study, the map, a new mark that might be the churchyard. "You stood there long enough to be a conversation. The land has a nose. I have a nose. I would rather my nose was first. You are ours. That is a fact I will defend against a stone."

"She said antlers."

Cairn goes still. Then he drinks. "Did she. File it. Don't hunt it. Antlers are a story they tell about kings. We have enough king-stories in this house and they all end badly." Dock-look. "The blood didn't take. Something else is awake. I will not put it in a lecture. I will use what I have. Candle at nine. Smaller. If you can feel the stone from this room we have a problem."

He sits. He feels the candle. He feels the house. He does not feel the stone, not from here, and Cairn's mouth does a thing that is almost relief. Priya puts her head in with tea for the unnamed man only. Joss, passing, says, "You smell like a churchyard," and it is not quite an insult, and Drew says, "Don't bring a witch home, they don't like the curtains," and Priya says, "You don't like the curtains," and the table, later, holds.

The land noticed what woke. That is weather. It is not a conversion. It is not the Deal. He does not know the Deal. He eats. The milk at the wall is a fact in the city. He does not make a religion. The crow is on the hedge. He almost tells it about Ruth. He does not. He says, not tonight, not even a whisper, only a thought, and the crow stays at the edge, which is all it is allowed, yet.
