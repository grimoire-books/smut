# Chapter 20
## Members

The war is elsewhere. That is a sentence Cairn uses, and it is not always a lie.

Saturday. Priya cooks like a siege that has been upgraded to an occupation. Drew's record player is a personality. Joss steals toast and puts it back before Priya speaks. The unnamed man is in the room as a member, not a lesson. Nobody hands him a timetable. Nobody rings a bell. The hardship is in the way Joss stands in a doorway until he turns sideways, and in the way Cairn's eye counts whether he flinched, and in the way Priya's kindness is a religion that still requires him to eat.

A guest of Drew's comes and goes smiling, pale. The guest is not a seminar. The guest is how the house eats when the house is a house. Drew waves from the stairs, mine-mine-mine in the wave, and the guest goes out into Palatine as if they have been to a good party. Priya watches the door after, and the watch is not cruel, and it is not free.

"You're staring," Joss says.

"I'm in the room."

"That's what I mean." Joss's smile is a clean knife that has decided, tonight, not to cut. "Cairn thinks you're coming along. Coming along is a horrible phrase. It means he hasn't broken you and he hasn't lost you. Congratulations. You're a member."

"I didn't apply."

"Nobody applies. That's the joke." Joss puts a glass in his hand that is not the dark stuff. "Drink. That's not the curriculum. That's Saturday."

He drinks. The house contains both temperatures. Drew talks about a boy in Ancoats who said thank you. Priya tells him to shut up. Cairn, from the study door, the map, the war that is elsewhere and also not, says, "Don't call him the hole in my hearing," and Joss says, "What do I call him then," and nobody has a name, and the radio fills it, and that is allowed.

Later Joss finds him in the hall, not blocking, which is new.

"Monday," Joss says. "He'll have you in the cellar. Not the study. The cellar. It's uglier and it isn't about coming. It's about standing when you want to sit. I will be there. I am the ugly part. You knew that."

"Aye."

"Don't aye me like a victim. You're still here. That's a choice. I don't do choices for people. I do the standing." Joss's mouth, brief, not a spit. "Sleep. If you dream about the chair I will be annoyed. Dream about the cellar. It's more honest."

He sleeps in the back room. He does not dream about the cellar. He dreams the table, which is a Priya thought, and he hates that, and he sleeps anyway, and in the morning there is toast, and butter, and Joss civil, which is worse, and Cairn saying, "Cellar at nine. Wear something you can ruin."

Priya looks at Cairn as if she might argue and then does not. She puts a jumper on the unnamed man's chair. "Eat. If he brings you back with a hole I will put a hole in him."

"Noted," Cairn says, and almost smiles, and the unnamed man eats, because the house is a table, and the bag is in the back room, and the chip-mug is in another life that has thinned like rain, and he is a member, which is not a diploma, which is a plate and a doorway and a cellar at nine.
