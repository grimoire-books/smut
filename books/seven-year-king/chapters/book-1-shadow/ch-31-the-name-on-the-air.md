# Chapter 31
## The Name on the Air

The name arrives sideways.

Not in the kitchen. Not in Cairn's mouth. On a wet pavement after a job that was not a mess, only a walk — papers, a door, a man who blinked. He is coming back toward the bus when a woman passes him. Not the train. Not Ruth. Coat. Dark hair. She says it like weather — Morrígan — as if she were naming a pub, or a station, and she is gone before he can ask who she is.

He does not follow.

The seed already had three sentences he could not get back. This is a fourth he will not give a news page. Names are armour until the house claims one. The house has not. A woman on a pavement does not get to.

He stands where he is until the pavement is only a pavement. Then he walks. The bus comes. He takes it. Palatine. He wipes his feet.

Table. Priya looks at his face and does not ask. Joss looks at his face and does.

"What."

"Nothing."

"That's a lie you tell when you've seen a bird."

"It isn't a bird."

Joss does not believe him and lets it go because Cairn is in the doorway.

Cairn: "The land is turning its head. You will not go and meet it without me. Eat."

He eats. The name sits in him like the look on the train. He does not follow that either. Following is how you end up in a story with a date on it. Ruth said. He files the name next to the look next to the crow that is sometimes only a crow.

In the back room he waits to see if the crow will be on the hedge. It is not. The hedge is a hedge. He sleeps. He does not dream her. He dreams doors shutting, which is almost the same and is not.

Morning the radio does a piece about crows in the city, too many, a man on the phone calling them a nuisance. Priya turns it off. He does not tell her why that was almost funny.

Joss, at the toaster: "If a woman on a pavement named you I would like to know, because I am the jealous sort and I am trying to be house-trained."

"She didn't name me."

"Good." Joss puts toast down. "Don't go collecting names off streets. We have enough furniture."

Cairn, from the study door: "He will not. Eat."

He eats. The name stays on the air and does not come in. He does not say it at the table. He does not say it to the hedge. He does not write it down. That is the whole of the courtesy he is offering a weather system that already has too many rooms.
