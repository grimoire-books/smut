# Chapter 31
## The Name on the Air

Ruth is on Palatine with the tote. He has a bag of shopping. She looks at him the way she looked at him after the study, like a warning, and then she talks too much.

"Don't panic. I'm not converting you. I wanted you to know — people are saying the land's in you. That's a sentence. I don't like it. There's an older word for what burns a taking out of a body and I am not going to make a religion of it in the street. The Morrígan. There. I've said it. Folklore department. Battle-crow. Washer. People use it when they mean a bad night and a bird. Don't follow it. Don't go waiting for trains. Your man would hate that and he would be right. I'm nervous. I talk."

The name sits in the air. He does not go toward it. He does not go to Victoria. He does not look at the crow on the hedge as if the crow had a name.

"I'm ours," he says.

"I know. That's why I'm being polite." She almost puts a hand on his arm and does not. "If you follow that word you will not come back with the right milk. Come home."

He comes home. Tells Cairn, because not telling is how you get converted by accident.

Cairn's face does the tired thing. "A folklore word. A bird. Do not hunt it. Do not let a woman with a tote give you a quest. You are ours. That is still not a romance. It is a fact I will defend against a name."

"Aye."

He does not follow. The temperature is opaque. Predator. Weather. Not a girlfriend. He puts the shopping down. Priya's radio. Joss stealing toast. Drew saying mine about a yoghurt. The name is in the street behind him. He leaves it there.

He sleeps. He does not dream a woman. He dreams a crow on a hedge, which is only a crow, and he does not nod, and he wakes, and he is still ours, and the name is a seed he does not water, and that is the whole of the sideways.
