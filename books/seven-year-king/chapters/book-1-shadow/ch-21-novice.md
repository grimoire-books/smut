# Chapter 21
## Novice

Cairn makes the week visible.

Not a timetable on the fridge. A look. A car. Joss in the passenger seat with his feet on the dash until Cairn looks at him, and then not.

Candle. Stand-behind. Joss. Table. Not the pub. Not Ruth's green. Not the study twice. Nights he is furniture. Nights he is the hand. He is still meat. He is less surprised by it.

Joss is the instrument. Cairn designs. That sentence is not said. It is the week.

Hulme. A terrace that has given up being proud of itself. Another house, not Vale. A man in a kitchen who will not stop talking — names, debts, a woman in Chorlton, a night he saw something in a canal and did not walk away. The tap left on like a light.

"Behind me," Cairn says.

He stands behind. He looks. He does not drink. He does not go in.

Cairn looks at the man. Then he looks at him. The air does the latch thing the study taught him from the other side. BIND. A count of ten. The talking stops as if someone had taken the plug out. The man's mouth is still open. Nothing comes out. Cairn finishes what he came for: a word, a look, a paper signed that is not a paper. Lets go. The man blinks and does not know what he lost. He knows he is tired.

In the car Joss says, "Pretty."

Cairn: "Useful. Do not confuse them."

Joss looks at him in the mirror. "He didn't smash it."

"He stayed in the room. That is the work." Cairn does not turn. "You will not take him in this car. You will not call him pretty when he has just been a tool. He is still meat. Meat that can count to ten is not a graduate."

Outsider to peer is not a ceremony. A man in Ancoats nodding at him instead of at Cairn. Priya handing him the list without the speech. Drew saying ours at the door and meaning the four of them and him. A woman at the corner shop who used to look through him and now looks away, which is Manchester manners learning a new fact.

He still goes to the warehouse. Mark still calls four early. The packing-line woman still will not stand next to him. She does not say why. A kettle — a kettle — goes down the roller and he tapes the box and does not think about the word. Soon work is a crime scene. Not yet. Smaller.

At the table Cairn says, "You are not a guest. Guests leave plates. You will wash. You will stand where I put you. You will not mistake Joss for the design. He is a blade. I hold the handle."

Joss shows his teeth at the toast. Priya does not laugh. He washes the plates.

Later Joss finds him at the sink and does not put a hand down his jeans.

"You're learning the handle," Joss says. "Don't get ideas. You're still the work."

"I know."

"Say the word if you need it. I will hate you. I will still stop. That's Priya. That's not Cairn. Don't get those mixed up either."

He dries a plate. In the back room he sits with the canal stone. Warm. Not as warm as the field. He does the candle smaller. He does not reach Hulme. He does not reach the field. He reaches the kitchen, the radio, Joss's door ajar, Priya's pencil on the sill. Stay in the room. The room is this house. He is in it. Still meat. Less surprised.

The week has a shape. That is the novice, if it needs a word. It does not.
