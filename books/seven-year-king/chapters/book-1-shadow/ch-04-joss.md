# Chapter 4
## Joss

The evenings acquire a shape.

Warehouse until four, or six. Bus. Palatine. The hedge. The door that opens before he knocks. Tea.

He is not moved in. The damp carpet still exists. He has not told the flatmate anything.

He leaves a hoodie on a peg because Joss takes it off his shoulders and hangs it there. Priya puts a mug in his hand.

Cairn talks in scraps. There is a current under the city. There are people who drink it out of other people. There are people who pull it out of parks and stones, and those are a different problem, and not tonight. Drew says *vampire* like a joke that has decided to be true. Cairn says *we*. Priya says *the house*.

Thursday he is late because the bus is a philosophy again.

No Priya. No Drew. Cairn's study door shut. A note on the table:

*Don't break him. — C*

Joss is in the hall. He leans on the banister as if he grew there.

"Shoes off."

He takes them off.

Joss comes off the banister. Close. He is not taller. He stands as if he is.

"You still stink of the park. I thought it would wash off. It hasn't." He tips his head. "Cairn thinks you're a hole we can point. Priya thinks you're a stray. Drew thinks you're a toy he's not allowed to unwrap. I think you're a park with a pulse and no house on it. You could have broken my hand in the kitchen and didn't."

"You could leave it."

"I don't like unfinished work."

A hand in the front of his shirt. Two steps. Wall by the coats. A placement. Joss smells of other people's cigarettes and Priya's soap.

"No," he says.

She hands him a cloth. He wipes his mouth. Pasta. Garlic. Drew comes in and says, "What did I miss," and Priya says, "Don't."

Cairn comes home at ten and looks at all of them and knows. At the door:

"He will push until you break or until you learn to hold. I need you not broken. Come tomorrow."

"I might not."

"You will." Like weather. "What he did is the house. The work is whether you come back knowing that."

On the bus his mouth still knows the shape. He showers until the water is only rust. He goes to work. He does not drop the kettles.

At four he looks at the other bus and does not take it.
