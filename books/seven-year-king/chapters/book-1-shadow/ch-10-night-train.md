# Chapter 10
## Night Train

He still does shifts. Class does not clock out because you have a back room in Palatine. Tuesday a late one, Mark's revenge for the soul being part-time. The last bus is a night bus in philosophy and a train in practice: tram, then a walk, then the little Northern train that smells of metal and wet wool if you miss the tram, which he does.

Victoria. Late. A man asleep on a bench with his mouth open. A cleaner with a radio. The board flickers. He stands where the wind comes down the platform and eats a pasty that has given up being hot. The leak is small. Cairn would call it progress. He calls it Tuesday.

She is further down the platform.

A woman. Not young, not old. A coat that does not care about fashion. She is washing nothing; there is no river; this is a train. She is only standing. He looks because the leak looks, the way it looked at Sian, at the girl on the sofa, at Joss's mouth. The air around her does not move with the station's air. He thinks: Vale. Another house. He thinks: don't be a hero.

He does not go to her. She looks up.

It is not a face he has. It is the pressure of having been seen, which he knows from a dream he has not kept. River-iron, without the river. He feels the seed sit up. His left shoulder tries to leave. He keeps it. Ten seconds. He stays on his patch of platform with a pasty and a cracked phone and does not walk. The pasty is grease in a paper. The cleaner’s radio is a song from before he was born. A man on the opposite platform shouts at a delay as if delays can hear. All of that is true at the same time as she is true.

She does not come over. She does not smile. She looks at him the way a person looks at a time, not a man. Not kind. Not unkind. A schedule. He almost lifts a hand, which would be a nod to a crow dressed as a greeting, and he does not. He could cross the platform in six steps. He could ask a name. He could wreck this night the way he could wreck Joss and has not. He files the not-doing next to the others. The yet, again.

A train comes that is not his. She gets on it. For a second she is only a coat in a door. The doors do a little chime. She does not look back. The air on the platform moves again, ordinary, diesel, wet wool. The leak leans after the train like a stupid dog. He holds it in the room. The room is a platform. The candle, tonight, is a pasty.

He does not follow. He does not have a word. He does not tell Cairn, later, because Cairn said keep the dream, and this feels like the dream wearing a coat. He gets his own train. He sits. His reflection is a man who did not move. A crow in the carriage at the far end, which is not allowed, which is a bird that got on at a station, which happens. It looks at him. He does not nod. He says, under his breath, "Not tonight," and a lad with headphones glances over, and he pretends he was coughing. The crow rides three stops and gets off at a nowhere, which is either a bird or a lesson. He does not decide.

Priya is up when he gets in, radio, tea, the siege-feeding look.

"You're late."

"Missed the tram."

"You look like you've seen a priest."

"Train." He takes the tea. He almost tells her. He tells her, instead: "I still have a house. The damp one. I should pay them something. It's not fair, the deposit."

"We'll sort it." We. She does not flinch at we. "Sleep. Cairn wants you in the back room at nine, candle, again. You're getting less sloppy. Don't get proud. Proud leaks."

He sleeps in the back room. He does not dream the woman on the platform. He dreams, stupidly, the pasty, which is his head having taste for once. In the morning Joss is civil, Drew says mine about the last yoghurt, Cairn says again, and the unnamed man feels only the candle for a whole minute, and Cairn does not praise him, which is praise.

He looks up trains north, once, in the warehouse toilet, not knowing why. Derry. Strabane. Nothing. He puts the phone away. Mark bangs the door. "You living in there."

"Aye."

"Funny. Aisle four. Kettles."

He does not drop them. The joke is still a joke. The woman on the platform is a stain, not a picture, same as the first dream, and he lets it rot, because that is the instruction he was never given and is following anyway.
