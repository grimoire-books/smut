# Chapter 18
## The Table

Morning is the table.

Butter. Radio. Priya does not look at his wrists because there is nothing to look at. She looks at his face. She puts toast down. Joss is civil in a shirt. Drew is not up. Cairn is at the head with the paper that is not a paper.

He sits. He eats.

Nobody says study. Nobody says no. Nobody says kettles. The house contains last night the way it contains the hall and the sink and the girl on the sofa: a room that happened, still here.

The tea tastes of the mug. His hands look like his hands. The rope is on the desk behind a door that is not ajar this morning. He does not go and check. Checking would be a religion.

Priya, to the toaster: "You're up. Good. I need the good plates washed. I am not asking Joss."

He washes the plates. Ordinary. His hands in the sink. The water is only water. Joss comes to stand in the doorway and does not touch him. That is not kindness. That is Cairn's look from the head of the table, travelling.

Cairn turns a page.

Once is not a habit. Cairn said that. He holds it in his mouth with the tea. The body has other ideas. The body leaned toward a door that was ajar and will lean again. He eats the toast anyway. Eating is a vote he still knows how to cast.

Drew comes down late and looks at all of them and says, "Still ours then," too loud, and Priya tells him to sit down and eat, and he sits, and the table holds.

Joss, not looking at anyone: "I'm going out."

"Be civil," Priya says.

"I'm always civil at breakfast." He is gone. The door shuts like a door.

Cairn does not look up. "Curriculum continues. You will stand behind me tonight. You will sit with the candle. You will not make last night into a reason to leave and you will not make it into a shrine. Wash. Eat. Walk if you have to walk. Come back."

"Aye."

"That is not a vow. That is a timetable."

He goes to the warehouse because the warehouse still exists. Mark says he looks like death. He packs. The kettles stay kettles — cardboard, tape, aisle four — and he almost laughs in the aisle, which would have been a different poverty. The packing-line woman will not stand next to him. He does not make her. At four he looks at the other bus and does not take it.

Palatine. Green door. The door opens before he knocks. He wipes his feet.

Priya: "You came back."

"Aye."

"Good. I saved you a plate. It will be worse tomorrow if you start skipping."

He eats the plate. Drew's knee finds his under the wood and stays, not a speech. The radio does a weather that is only weather. Cairn's study door is shut. The rope is on the other side of it. The table is this side.

Still theirs. Still meat. Still a table.

He sleeps in the back room. The flinch comes when a floorboard in the hall sounds like a door. He waits it down. He does not go and look. Looking would be a habit. Once is not a habit. He holds that until it is almost true.

That is the whole morning after. It is also the evening. The table does not comment. That is why it is the table.
