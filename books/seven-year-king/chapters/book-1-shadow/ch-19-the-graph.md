# Chapter 19
## The Graph

Morning is a graph.

He learns it without meaning to. Night is worse: the wrist, the chair, the extra beat of Cairn's hand. Four o'clock is less. The study door moving is a flinch in the neck that he hates more than the memory. Priya's radio is almost nothing. Joss's fingers in his hair on the sofa are a lean, not a grab, and he lets them, because Joss stopped, and the stopping is a fact he is allowed to keep.

He eats jam that is too sweet. He does the candle at nine. Only the candle. Cairn in the room, not touching. "Useful," Cairn says, which is the dock, and he walks out before the word can become a kindness.

The curriculum does not pause. That is the point of a curriculum. Cairn takes him out on a Thursday that has no right to be ordinary. A closed tap in a stairwell in Salford. The man in the kitchen looks at Cairn and nods and shuts it himself. No rope. No chair. The unnamed man stands behind. He does not pull. His hands work, after, on a wall in the wet, and Cairn waits with him and does not speak, and that is not aftercare and is not not.

"Still standing," Cairn says, in the car.

"Aye."

"Good. The blood is not tonight. Do not look at me like that. I said not tonight."

He does not look like that. He looks like a man who has been opened and has got up and is being driven back to Palatine to eat pasta. The house required it. He believes that. The belief sits next to the hate and neither of them wins.

Priya puts a plate down. She does not ask if he is a tap. Joss, at the counter, not eating, says, "You don't have to be useful today."

"I do. That's living here."

Joss's mouth is a line. "I will not be civil to him."

"That's your marriage," the unnamed man says, and it is almost the old sport, and it dies, and Priya turns the radio up one notch, which is mercy.

He sleeps in the back room. Once he sleeps in Joss's bed because Joss asks, please, no belt, and he says aye, and they do not fuck, and Joss's arm is careful, and in the night he flinches at a hand on his wrist that is only Joss asleep, and Joss wakes and takes the hand away without being told, and that, too, is the house.

The crow is on the hedge. He does not tell it. He does not tell it about the chair. He does not tell it about the extra beat. He thinks, once, of a woman on a platform who looked at him like a time, and he does not go to Victoria. He puts a plate on his knee. He is useful. He hates Cairn in the mornings and less at four and more at night, which is not forgiveness, it is a graph. He does not tidy the graph.

Cairn, at the study door, a week on: "I will not do that again. Once is the point. Twice is a habit. I am not building a habit of that. I am building a man who can stand. If the door opens I will find another way. I will not tell you it was for your own good. It was for the house. You may leave. I would rather you didn't."

"I'm not leaving."

"I know." Cairn's tired face. "That's the leash. I am not proud of it. I am using it. Both of those can sit too."

They sit. The radio. The map. The seed, not a river tonight: a chair, a table, a crow, a man who said no and came anyway and got up in the morning, which is not a moral, which is a life.

Mark, if Mark still existed, would say he looked like shite. Mark does not exist in this kitchen. The hi-vis is in a bag in the damp house he no longer pays for, or pays for as a ghost. Palatine smells like polish and old blood and pasta. He knows which way he walks at four. He files that. He does not tell Cairn. He picks up a tea towel because Priya's hands are full. He does not drop it.

The crow is on the hedge. He does not nod. He is learning.

Days, not a montage: a flinch when Drew says *stay* in the kitchen, laughing, and Drew's face falling, and Priya saying, later, "He knows enough, not all of it." A flinch when Joss's fingers find his hair, and then he leans. Asked taking: none, for a week. The not-asking is its own temperature. He is still ours. He still eats. He still stands behind Cairn once more, a different stairwell, a different nod, and after he has to sit on a wall again, and Cairn waits again, and the graph does another little line.

Ruth, at the corner, tote, sees his face and does not talk too much, which is how he knows he looks like a warning. She says, "I'm sorry," without knowing, and he says, "Don't be," and she says, "The stone's quiet. That's not always a kindness," and he walks home, and home is the table, and the table is a collar, and both are true, and neither of them is the turning, and he knows that without having the word, because Cairn said the blood is later, and later is a country he has not entered, and the crow still has not been told.
