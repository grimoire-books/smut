# Chapter 19
## The Graph

The study sits in the house after.

Not a speech. A flinch when Cairn's door is ajar. A lean toward it anyway, which is worse. He does the candle in the back room without being told. Smaller. Stay in the room. The rope is on the desk behind a door he does not open.

The flinch has a shape now. He could draw it if he had a pencil and the kind of honesty that survives toast.

Morning: the study door moves and his neck does a thing. Hate. Heat under the hate, traitor, the body remembering the command. He eats jam that is too sweet and does not look at Cairn's hands.

Afternoon: Tesco list on the fridge. Milk. Bread. Tomatoes that have never seen a vine. He takes the list because standing still is worse. In the cereal aisle a woman reaches past him for Weetabix and her sleeve brushes his wrist and he has to put the basket down and wait. The flinch comes and goes. He pays. He walks back under the hedge. The crow is not on it. He does not tell the house about the train. He does not tell them about a look that had a date in it.

Evening: Joss's fingers in his hair on the sofa. He flinches. Then he leans, because Joss stopped in a room that was not this one, and the leaning is a vote he is allowed. Joss feels the flinch and the lean both and does not make a joke. That is new. Or it is Cairn's look from the study door, travelling.

Night: he puts his own hand on his own wrist in the dark and the body answers as if the hand were Cairn's. He takes the hand off. He waits both things down — the heat, the hate — and does not make either of them a vote. Arousal is not a vote. He already knew. He knows it again, with better furniture.

Cairn finds him at the table with the radio on.

"Curriculum continues. You will stand behind me. You will sit with the candle. You will not make last night into a religion or a reason to leave. Eat."

He eats. The tea tastes of the mug. Joss watches him wash a plate and does not put him on his knees.

Priya, later, in the back room doorway, not coming in: "You don't have to be useful today."

"I do."

"That's his sentence."

"It's also mine." He hates that. He says it anyway. "I'm not leaving."

"I know." She does not offer the word. The word is for Joss. The word was said in the study and did nothing. She knows. The house is small. "Eat something that isn't a dare. Then sleep. The graph can wait until morning."

He sleeps. He gets up. There is no warehouse now; that door thinned. He walks Palatine because walking is a sentence he still knows. At four he looks at the other bus out of habit and does not take it. The door opens before he knocks. He wipes his feet.

Once is not a habit. He holds that in his mouth with the tea. He leans anyway.

The crow is on the hedge at first light and gone by the toast. He does not nod. He does not tell it. The graph has three points and a line he will not name: hate in the morning, less at four, more at night. It is not forgiveness. It is a week. The table holds. He is still ours. He still does not have a name. He still gets up.
