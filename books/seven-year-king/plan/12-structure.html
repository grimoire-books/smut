12. Structure

From Planning Bible · Draft 07. Canonical whole: PLANNING-BIBLE.md. Word source: material/source/Seven_Year_King_Planning_Bible.docx.

Locked decisions are marked LOCK. Open choices are marked OPEN.

Three books, three allegiances, one slow burn. Surface plot is the story we already have. The books are the moral camera. Lived time about three to four years from dream to death across the three volumes. Year-stamps so the reader counts toward the named end-date. After the pledge the remaining years exist as a future he talks about. They do not get written. That theft is the point.

What each book is allowed to believe

  • Book 1: the house is home. The Shadow King is a rumour. Danu looks like rescue. Reader should want the nest to win, then feel the tree. No lecture.
  • Book 2: the land is life. The war is the greater good. Reader signs the bill with him. Innocents and enemy-lovers and sacrifice all happen while that belief is sincere.
  • Book 3: both flags were costumes. Simplification was the crime. He is her champion in the open. Still a war and a love story --- not a systems lecture. The ethic is felt when he refuses the throne.

Hinges

Book 1 ends on the Deal. Book 2 ends on the grief / the second ledger. Book 3 ends on the crow. Prologue opens Book 1. Crow ends Book 3 only. Do not put her interiority at the end of 1 or 2.

Lived time inside the three books

The year-map below still holds. Book 1 is pre-pledge. The seven-year clock starts at the hinge into Book 2. Death is Book 3, early. Do not make each book one calendar year.

Order of the two killings

Root vampire first (Book 2), Goddess second (Book 3), with the volume break as aftermath. The root-kill is the personal tragedy and Book 2's ending charge. Killing the Goddess is the theological tragedy and the refusal of a third throne. He kills dictators, not realms. If both monarchs fall in one night the reader does not sit with the loss, the growth has no time, and the last night has no rhyme.

What "hidden at the outside" means in practice

Y0 through the run-up to the Deal should be readable as a dark urban fantasy with vampires, shifters, sex and a mysterious dream. A reader who does not know Sláine should not feel they are missing homework. The Deal pays the cheque the prologue wrote. The early death pays a cheque the Deal did not know it was writing.

Do not telegraph

Narration does not hint that he will miss year 7. He counts. He plans. The lovers plan. The only fair clue already given is the prologue: she spends them at the best time, not on a lease.
