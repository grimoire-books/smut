PLANNING BIBLE · DRAFT 07

THE SEVEN-YEAR KING

A smut epic fantasy set in modern Britain and Ireland

Working title. Alternatives at the end of this document.

This is a planning document, not a draft of the novel. It digests the source influences, resolves them into one cosmology and one magic system, and lays out structure, character ladders, the prologue, the seven-year geis, sex and violence design, and the questions that still need answering before a word of the book is written. Draft 02 amended the clock: seven years is the expectation at the pledge, not the runtime of his death. Draft 03 locked the deeper plot: the Morrígan never pretends the leash is love. Draft 04 locked the cascade as grief and willing sacrifice. Draft 05 locked the war and the full emotional range. Draft 06 locked power exchange and consent. Draft 07 locks the deep structure: good is a complex system; both factions are dictators trying to simplify it; the work is a trilogy --- shadow, then Green, then Morrígan.

Locked decisions are marked LOCK. Open choices are marked OPEN.

Prepared from the author's brief plus research into Sláine: The Horned God (Pat Mills / 2000 AD), the Morrígan and sacral kingship, Eragon, Trudi Canavan's Black Magician trilogy, Hannaford Prep, House Isador, Forged in Blood, Twilight, True Blood, Tristan's Tale (IncomingPornDuck), and Endangered (ltpc).

Status: planning only. No prose. Draft 07 --- trilogy spine and complexity ethic.

1. One-page premise

A young man in modern Britain and Ireland wakes from a dream in which he was both a dying warrior and the crow circling above him. He does not yet have a name on the page. He does not yet know he has been chosen.

He falls first among vampires: beautiful, violent, and apparently the good-ish side of a hidden war. They take him in. They open him. They make him powerful and they make him theirs. The sex is the leash and the education. He climbs a control ladder from prey to equal to the one holding the bond.

Then the other current finds him. Nature-witches, land-bound shapeshifters, standing stones that hum when he walks past. An Earth Goddess who offers what every sacred king has ever been offered: power now, death on a date he will know in advance. He takes the deal. Seven years. He will become the Horned God. At the moment of pledging, he and the reader are fully bought into that term. He names the end-date. He makes a plan that requires year six.

The war looks clean for a while. Green against shadow. Life against hunger. The truth is neither side wants balance. The Earth Goddess is pulling all life-force out of the Otherworld and hoarding it here. The Shadow King --- last cycle's Horned God, who refused his grave and tried to make the term infinite --- is pulling it back. Vampires are not a species. They are one broken king's hunger, subdivided. When the root dies, the whole tree dies, including the people the protagonist has loved.

He kills the root while still her champion. The people he loves have seen the tree. They know the kill will take them. They are willing and afraid. It is a sacrifice, not a massacre. The rest of the line dies without a choice. He lives in both rooms. The center of the book is grief --- stay in it, feel the guilt --- and then that grief turns into strength. Their death is a rehearsal of his own. He defects because their sacrifice must not become fertilizer for Danu. The geis comes due early. On the last night the Morrígan comes to him as a woman --- not because the calendar ended, because the campaign did. She teaches him how to spend the unlived years. He already watched people he loved do this. Now he can match them. He burns the years, kills the Goddess, dies mid-term. The book ends in her eyes, in crow form, on his body, in a real place, at dawn.

All the deities are ultimately aspects of the Earth Goddess --- until they are not. The Morrígan is the incarnation of death and battle. She does not serve the hoard. She serves the cycle. He was always hers.

2. What the book is actually about

On the surface: sex, violence, hidden gods, a man becoming a monster and then a god and then a corpse. Some of the sex is a real exchange of power --- sometimes he asks, sometimes he does not get to.

Underneath: whether love can tell the truth about cost. Every major relationship in the book is a negotiation about who holds the leash. The vampires dress the collar as found family. Danu dresses the harvest as marriage. The Shadow King dresses hunger as home. The Morrígan never pretends the leash is love. The leash is the job. The love is real and separate. She can hold both. That is her strength, and it is the strength she requires of her champions.

She is the messy, morally correct centre of the book. Messy at the scale of a body --- people she loves die, and she watches. Correct at the scale of two realms --- neither hoard wins, both worlds keep existing. She does what the greater good requires, always. She is the only big deity figure who actually loves persons. She sacrifices what she loves with her eyes open. She has the strength to bear it. So do the ones who say yes.

This is the ultimate slow burn. It takes the whole epic to see it. If the reader finishes the prologue thinking she is the villain, good. If they spend the nest years thinking Danu will be the beloved, good. The thesis arrives when he does --- on the last night --- and not as a speech. Sex and violence are the surface. This is what everything is built toward.

The moral engine is not good versus evil as team jerseys. Simplification is the crime. Complexity is the good. Consent is how a complex system stays alive at the scale of a body. Two dictators are trying to flatten a living two-realm system into one reservoir. He kills the dictators, not the worlds. He dies so he is not the third. The crow continues the work without sitting a throne.

Deep structure --- the undercurrent

This is not surface plot. This is what the plot is doing when it thinks it is doing something else.

  • Good = structuring and balancing a complex system. Messy. Many agents. Feedback. Both realms fed and loud. Boost both. Extinguish neither.
  • Bad = unbalancing and destroying that complexity. Collapsing it toward one pole, one will, one reservoir.
  • The two extremes that call themselves good and bad are the same crime in different flags. Danu simplifies into summer. The Shadow King simplifies into night. Each is a dictatorial authority. Each turned a realm into a monopoly.
  • The shadow realm was only turned to evil. It is supposed to be the other lung --- source, winter, death-that-feeds-return, the far side of the valve.
  • By the end we realise the material realm was turned too. Danu's life is a dictatorship of summer. Harvest dressed as health.
  • Real good lives in three conditions: no ultimate dictatorial authority; free consensual exchanges; a complex system that can hold a lot of both.
  • Same thesis at three scales. Body: forced orgasm is one will taking a climax; last night is two wills naming a morning. War: innocents and enemy-lovers are a system being simplified by a campaign. Cosmos: Aether drawn only one way is a realm being starved.
  • It is a trilogy. Part 1 he is shadow. Part 2 he is Earth Goddess. Part 3 he is Morrígan --- in ethic and allegiance, not in office. She outlasts him.

Tone contract

  • Epic fantasy scale (realms, gods, a war that predates cities) crashing constantly into banal modern Britain and Ireland: trains, wet streets, cheap lager, a Greggs at dawn after a killing.
  • Smut that is load-bearing, not garnish. Sex is how power moves. If a sex scene could be cut without changing who holds the leash, it is a side-story and should be labelled as such.
  • Body-horror sitting next to heat. The warp-spasm is not a hot super-mode. It is the Horned God arriving in a human body that was not built for it. The reader should want him and be a little afraid of wanting him.
  • Violence that is passionate, not procedural. The prologue sets this: battle as sex's twin, not its opposite.
  • Slow-burn main attachments; frequent side-sex so the book never goes cold waiting for the romance to ripen.
  • Full range of human emotion at the extremes. The book is allowed to be funny and horny and tender on Tuesday and unendurable on Thursday. If every chapter after year one is grim there are no extremes --- only one note, loud.
  • No punches pulled on cost. Stay in the room. Not a catalogue of atrocities. Few innocents, few enemy-lovers, go all the way down.

3. Influence digest — steal / discard

Each source is treated as a parts bin, not a template. The finished book should not be recognisable as a remix of any one of them.

Sláine: The Horned God --- Pat Mills / Simon Bisley

The mythic frame of the entire novel. A Celtic warrior becomes consort to the Earth Goddess Danu and incarnation of Carnun, the Horned God. The Sessair king reigns exactly seven years, then is ritually killed so his earth-power returns to the land. Slough Feg is the previous Horned God who refused to die, ate Time Worm eggs to prolong life, and rotted into a tyrant. Danu is already not purely good in the source: playful, cruel, fond of word-games, approving of ritual death because "death is only a pause."

Steal

  • Seven-year sacred kingship as a job, not a punishment.
  • The king as husband of the land. Sex with the goddess or her avatar is a sovereignty rite.
  • Warp-spasm / ríastrad: earth-power warping the body from the inside. Ugly. Monstrous. Not fully under his control. First triggered, in Sláine, by childhood bullying.
  • Slough Feg as the template for the Shadow King: the Horned God who said no.
  • Danu's moral slipperiness, held back until the late turn so the reader loves her first.
  • Matriarchal theology. He serves. He does not rule the goddess.

Discard

  • Tír na nÓg as the primary setting. This book is modern Britain and Ireland with the Otherworld alongside it.
  • Ukko the dwarf, Brainbiter as a named axe, the four tribal treasures as a collectathon --- usable later as texture, not plot engine.
  • Front-loading the Horned God. After the prologue peek, this material stays buried until the deal.

The Morrígan, Cú Chulainn, sacral kingship

Phantom Queen. War, fate, sovereignty. Triple aspect: Badb (crow, panic, prophecy), Macha (the land's right to choose its king), Nemain (the frenzy that drives men mad mid-fight). She does not swing a sword. She whispers into hearts and spends lives like a general spends battalions.

Before the Second Battle of Mag Tuired she couples with the Dagda at the river Unius and only then commits her strategy to his side. Sex is the treaty, not the reward. She offered herself to Cú Chulainn; he refused; she became his enemy in animal forms; when he died lashed to a standing stone she landed on his shoulder as a crow. That is the last word of their relationship.

Irish sacral kingship (Frazer, Mac Cana, the bog bodies): the king is spouse of the territory. Fixed term. Death is excessive by design so the sacrifice feeds the goddess in all her aspects. Nipple-cutting on bog kings was dethronement --- sucking a king's nipples was a gesture of submission; destroying them un-kinged him in this world and the next.

Steal

  • Whisper first, appear as a woman only on the final night, fuck, watch the death as a crow.
  • The Washer at the Ford, used in the prologue and again on the last night.
  • Oweynagat (Cave of the Cats, Rathcroghan) as her house and a gate.
  • Geis as the legal form of the seven-year deal.
  • The inversion of Cú Chulainn: these champions say yes. Outcome is the same. Meaning is not.

Discard

  • Morrígan-as-romantic-lead. She is not the girlfriend. She is the only being who tells him the truth, and the truth is always you will die and I will be there.
  • A textbook dump of the Ulster Cycle. One Irish-myth name early (hers). The rest earned.

Tristan's Tale --- IncomingPornDuck

Unfinished Literotica epic (12 parts, then deleted). Mind-control smut that grows into a secondary-world war. Readers stayed for the characters and the scale, not just the kink. The Art is mental magic working from the inside of a mind; Chi is the self externalised; Castles are shared lucid-dream constructs where practitioners meet, hide, fight, and rewrite people. Sex sits on the line between reptilian instinct and conscious intention --- the best attack surface because it is where people are most deluded about the control they think they have. The antagonist plants ideas in a culture until they become policy. LOTR-scale enemy that feels unbeatable because they work through people.

Steal

  • Mind-work as ENTER, not puppetry. You meet a mind in Aether and rewrite from inside. Cost is real: you risk your own Castle.
  • Sex as the gate between instinct and will.
  • The big bad as an idea-planter, not only a monster.
  • Giving the tools to more than one person so the book is not a one-way control fantasy.
  • He is controlled first. Then he learns the Art. Then he refuses to use it the way it was used on him. Then he uses it once at the end as chosen sacrifice.

Discard

  • The secondary-world isekai door-in-the-house. Our Otherworld is already here.
  • Unexamined non-con as the book's standing temperature. Early violation is diegetic and later answered.

Endangered --- ltpc, Literotica

A hidden dragon in modern America, raised by a witch, whose aspect is the Ether itself: "the nothing between everything," a sub-level of reality where everything in the Norm has a bleed-through shadow. Dragons overflow with magic. Sex can break concealment spells and trigger emergence. High-end work includes pocket-space, pressure and heat, matter creation. Witchcraft is attached to nature and is expensive to take into the Ether.

Steal

  • Aether as a place and a fuel. The protagonist's aspect is the between, not green and not shadow. That is why both sides want him.
  • Exceptional draw from the other realm, beyond what is normal.
  • Sex as the thing that cracks the hiding-spell on his nature.
  • Witch magic as land-work, slower and costlier than his raw draw.

Discard

  • Dragons as a species, the American farm, the harem-of-magical-women logistics, explicit dragon-cock transformation as the book's identity. If a dragon appears it is one being, late, and earned.

Eragon --- Inheritance Cycle

Magic is energy work. Cost equals the real-world physical work of the same act. Overdraw kills. Language is a focus tool, not the source; wordless magic exists and is more dangerous. True Names grant control over the named thing. Knowing your own True Name is self-mastery.

Steal

  • Conservation of energy as hard law. No free lunch. You pay with life, borrowed life, or landscape-life.
  • Wordless magic as advanced and unsafe.
  • True Name as a late-game identity beat: he learns his name as balance, not as either faction's champion.

Discard

  • A constructed language the reader must learn. Dragons as partners. Elves.

Black Magician trilogy --- Trudi Canavan

Sonea, slum-girl, bullied inside an elite magical guild. Akkarin, High Lord, looks like the villain for two books: he takes her as ward and hostage to protect a forbidden practice that is actually holding back an invasion. Higher/Black Magic means dropping the polite visualisation of a personal "source" and siphoning life-energy from other living beings. Willing gift versus theft is the moral line. She offers herself as a battery so he can stay alive. Love arrives late, after hate and fear. He dies. She finishes the fight with the last of his power.

Steal

  • The Akkarin figure for the team-vamp phase: older, grey, apparently the problem, actually fighting a war he will not name.
  • Protagonist starts as Sonea (bullied, hostage, battery) and becomes Akkarin (the one who holds the forbidden technique).
  • Systematic humiliation that does not break him; it forges control. The bully can later flip or stay as a wound.
  • Power-sharing as intimacy.

Discard

  • The Guild-as-boarding-school. Teenage students. A capital-city court of magicians. Anyone sexually active in this book is twenty or older.

Hannaford Prep --- J. Bree

Scholarship outsider at an elite school. Already a weapon ("the Wolf") pretending to be prey. The rich boys bully her; the slow burn turns them into lovers. Violence is real. Found family is the prize. The humiliation is a tactic she endures because blowing her cover would cost more than the bruises.

Steal

  • Already lethal, choosing to eat the humiliation. Do not write a helpless lamb. Write a weapon that has agreed, for now, to be sheathed.
  • Bully-to-lovers as one strand, not the whole book.
  • Found family that will be tested by the cascade.
  • Slow burn on the main attachments; the user is right that Hannaford is a bit too slow --- side-stories carry the heat early.

Discard

  • The school. The underage setting. Import the energy into an adult crucible: graduate scheme, City house, military mess, vampire-kept commune, elite Dublin or London set.

House Isador, Forged in Blood, Twilight

House Isador: a vampire queen and her Blood. Taking someone is love, feeding, mental access and power-share in one act. Devotion is both the kink and the politics. One existing Blood is already "the Morrígan's own Shadow" --- useful proof the two mythologies can share a universe.

Forged in Blood: the exact control ladder requested. Prey and abduction; blood-bond dressed as protection; possessive intimacy; he becomes the fulcrum; the bond is revealed as a leash belonging to someone older. Dark academia texture. A professor-sire who can read minds is a clean first-act Akkarin.

Twilight, stripped for parts: a human trait amplified into a unique mental gift on turning; a shield as late-game answer to the Art; shifter pack-mind and an Alpha voice that can force obedience.

Steal

  • The taking economy. Sex and blood are the same act.
  • The five-stage control ladder as the spine of the relationships.
  • Sire-line death cascade on TVD/Originals physics: only the root dying collapses the tree. Intermediate sires dying does nothing.
  • Amplified mental gifts. One shield.

Discard

  • Isis/Triune chess-court and a twelve-person harem. Sparkle, vegetarian moral high ground, imprint-as-destiny, YA chastity. Vampires in this book should feel like wet earth, old blood, and borrowed time.

True Blood + Stranger Things

Shifters: born, not bitten. First change under a hard moon. Any animal once imprinted; each person has a go-to form that costs almost nothing. Full moon is involuntary. Sleep dumps them back to human. Packs and territory. They stay secret longer than vampires.

Stranger Things contributes only the two-realms image: a world alongside this one, leaking, hungry, with a thing at the centre of it. Celtic cosmology already has this (the Otherworld is horizontal, not vertical) and is the better source. Do not steal the Demogorgon, the kids-on-bikes register, or Upside-Down-as-rot-copy.

Steal

  • Shifter rules as written above.
  • Three political slivers among the packs: land/green, city/shadow, Morrígan crow-kin.
  • The Otherworld as adjacent, entered by threshold not distance.

Discard

  • Coming-out-to-the-press vampire integration. Skinwalkers except as one villain, once.

4. Cosmology

Two realms, one reservoir

The Otherworld sits alongside this world, not under it and not in space. Separated by a threshold: sídhe mounds, caves, lakes, sea-mist, certain doors, Samhain and Beltane, extreme emotion, sex with a deity, death. Time can slip at the crossings. Death here is birth there, and the reverse. That exchange is the engine.

LOCK. One name for the fuel, used by the narrator and eventually by the protagonist: Aether. In-world slang splits by faction. Earth-side says the Green or earth-power. Shadow-side says the Hunger or the Dark. Do not also say Flow, Chi, mana, or the Force.

Aether is the life-force shared by both realms. Direction of draw is the whole war.

  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
                       **Earth-current**                       **Shadow-current**
  -------------------- --------------------------------------- -----------------------------------------
  Direction            Otherworld → here                       Here → Otherworld

  Feels like           Growth, sex, birth, green, warp-spasm   Frost, undeath, appetite, entropy

  They call it         Feeding the land                        Restoring the source

  It actually is       Starving the other side                 Starving this side

  Face                 The Earth Goddess (Danu / Anu)          The Shadow King (root vampire)

  Soldiers             Witches, land-packs, sacred kings       Vampires, demons, city-packs, bad magic

  Lie they tell        We are life                             We are the original home
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Neither dictator wants a living system. Both want the whole reservoir. The Earth Goddess is doing the same thing as the Shadow, in reverse. That is the late-trilogy realisation and it has to be earned. Until then she must look like Sláine's Danu --- mother of the land, enemy of the rot --- or the reader will smell the twist. The shadow realm was only turned. By the last book we see the material realm was turned too. Neither world is the enemy. The dictators are.

The Morrígan is the valve

Not a trickle between hoards. Bidirectional abundance. Feed winter and summer. Feed here and there. Champions are how she keeps the wheel from locking: no king keeps it. Every glorious death she stages returns a pulse of Aether the other way. She is not a third queen. She is the function that stops the system simplifying. For most of the trilogy she looks like Danu's war-aspect. By the end we understand she seceded, or always wore the aspect as cover. She never metabolises a corpse into the land required it. She never pretends the leash is love. Love-as-keeping is how systems flatten into one will.

What he kills, what he leaves

He kills both monarchs. He does not kill both realms. After they fall the Otherworld can stop being hell and be winter/source/return again. The Green can stop being a monopoly and be a living land. If Book 3 extinguishes either world we have written the crime we said we were against. He becomes the most powerful being in the universe and then dies so he does not become dictator number three. The death is the refusal of a throne. The crow continues the work without sitting it.

Origin of the vampires

LOCK. The root vampire is a remote throne, not a lover. Optional mythic identity, not stated early: he is last cycle's Horned God who refused the geis and took his stolen earth-power into the Shadow. That stolen power became the first hunger. He sired others to extend his reach into this world. Every vampire is a fragment of a broken sacred king. When he dies, the whole line collapses --- because they were never separate lives. They were his refusal, subdivided.

Bottom-level vampires do not know this. They think they are a culture. They fall in love. They are sincere. The love is real. The metaphysics are a lie. Intermediate sires dying does nothing to the tree. Only the root.

Do not make the cascade work for Earth-side champions or shifters. If killing the Goddess genocides the green as well, the ending flattens.

Naming discipline

  • Early chapters: "the Earth Goddess" --- what vampires and humans say.
  • Witches may say Danu or Anu once it is earned.
  • Do not introduce Carnun, Cernunnos, or Horned God as a proper title until the deal is on the table.
  • The Morrígan is named in the prologue and then almost not at all, until she is.
  • The Shadow King is spoken of the way people speak of a weather system or a distant chairman. He is not in the room until the end.

5. Magic system

Five verbs. Everything a gifted person does is one of these, or a combination.

  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  **Verb**    **What it is**
  ----------- ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  **DRAW**    Pull Aether through the self. Earth-side natives have a cap. Otherworld-touched have no cap except what the current will let through. The protagonist has a leak, not a tank. This is the Endangered / Eragon / Canavan merge.

  **SHAPE**   Impose will on matter and energy. Conservation applies. Lift a car, pay for lifting a car. Create a flame from nothing, pay the full thermal cost. High-end: pressure, heat, material manipulation, pocket-space.

  **BIND**    Share, steal, or gift life-force. Vampire feeding. Canavan higher magic. House Isador taking. A willing gift is different, morally and practically, from a theft. Repeatable siphon is how individuals become nuclear.

  **ENTER**   Project into Aether-space: dreams, Castles, the between. Mind-work lives here, not in SHAPE. You do not puppet a body like a rock. You meet a mind and rewrite from inside. Cost: you risk your own Castle. Fear makes alignment harder; intimacy makes it easier.

  **WARP**    The body becomes an overloaded conduit and distorts. Sláine's ríastrad. Early spasms are partial, ugly, terrifying. Late spasms grow antler, change bone, make him something other than human. Final form is the Horned God --- and that form is what dies. The Goddess can refuse the tap. After the deal, the Morrígan can open it.
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Laws

  • Conservation. You pay with life, borrowed life, or landscape-life. No free lunch.
  • Direction. Most beings can only work one current. Vampires BIND along the Shadow-current. Witches DRAW and SHAPE along the Earth-current. Shifters are split.
  • The protagonist exception. He can DRAW both ways. That is why both sides want him and why he can kill both.
  • Sex is a BIND + ENTER event. Orgasm drops the gate between instinct and will. The Morrígan's champion-night is the oldest ritual form of this.
  • Accepting the seven-year deal is speaking a True Name as sacrifice. The geis is a BIND on the self.

How it feels in the book

Earth-current work smells like wet soil, copper, sex, crushed green. Shadow-current work smells like cold iron, old rooms, the inside of a mouth after a nosebleed. Aether itself --- the between, his aspect --- has no smell. It is the pressure change before weather. People who stand near him when he DRAWs both ways get a nosebleed or an erection or both. That is a feature.

Gifts

From Twilight's usable part: a human trait can amplify into a unique mental gift when a person is opened to Aether (turned, sworn, or warped). One character gets a shield that answers the Art. One vampire gets emotion-push or sensory wipe. Do not give everyone a party trick. Three gifts in the whole book is plenty.

The Art, specifically

Ported from Tristan's Tale and parked under ENTER. Early on it is done to him. Mid-book he learns it and hates how easy sex makes it. Late-book he can do it without sex and chooses not to, except once. Castles can be nested. A stronger practitioner can suppress someone else's externalised self --- experienced as castration-of-self, not a stun-spell. The Shadow King works this way at cultural scale: plant a single idea until it becomes policy. Vampire courts and human institutions are his Castles.

Shapeshifters are the only faction honestly split. That is their narrative job: they make the "both sides" thesis visible before the protagonist can afford to see it. A land-pack girl and a city-pack boy can be in the same bed and still be on different currents.

Vampire texture

City infrastructure. Mayfair, Dublin Georgian squares, Edinburgh New Town, a peerage that never died. They begin as Forged in Blood / House Isador good-ish: territorial, sexual, protective, funny, violent. The reader should want to stay with them. The cascade only hurts if the reader has been on team vamp.

Earth-side texture

Travellers, certain rural families, university folklore departments that are not what they claim, a pub that has been on the same crossroads since before the Romans. Not druids in robes. People who still leave milk at a back door and could not tell you why.

7. The Morrígan

The only Irish-myth name for a long stretch. That is deliberate world-building and foreshadowing, per the brief.

She never pretends the leash is love.

That sentence is a design rule for every scene she is in. It is also the book's thesis. The leash is the job. The love is real and separate. She can hold both. Danu cannot. The Shadow King cannot. The nest cannot. That is why she is the only big deity figure who actually loves, and why it takes the whole novel to see it.

How she works

  • Whispers into the hearts and minds of men. Incites them to battle. Always, she will say, in the service of the Earth Goddess. This is the cover.
  • Occasionally raises a great warrior for a great destiny. Speaks to them slowly, gradually, building, until the final night.
  • On that night she appears as a woman and fucks them. The next day they die a glorious death, completely her servant.
  • They enter a battle frenzy, take many lives, accomplish one strategically essential thing, then succumb. She watches from above in crow form.
  • Always their death has meaning. She is a master of strategy. She spends her champions at the best time. Their glory is assured.

What the others pretend

  • Danu dresses the harvest as marriage. Husband of the land. Warm language. Ripen him, keep him till year 7, burst him into the hoard. The leash is called love.
  • The nest and the sire-tree dress hunger as family. Blood-bond, protection, what we take we keep. Sincere feeling inside a collar. The leash is called love.
  • The Shadow King dresses immortality as belonging, or as an end to hunger. Keep. Do not lose.

What she does instead

She separates the sentences and will not collapse them:

  • I will use you.
  • You will die.
  • I want you.

She never folds 3 into 1 to make 2 easier to swallow. Comfort would be a lie. Comfort is what the others sell. Honesty is the only form her love is allowed to take, which is why she is hard to love until the last page, and why that page is the slow-burn payoff of the entire book.

Morally correct, not morally pretty

Greater good = the valve. Both realms fed. No hoard. She does what that requires, always. Correctness is in the paying of her own costs, not in cleanliness. She likes them. She fucks them. She whispers them into knives. She is allowed to be cruel, hungry, tired, fond, even jealous for one uncontrolled flicker. She still will not lie to win him. She does not compete with the nest. She outlasts it.

Love, as this book argues for it: the willingness to lose what you love rather than corrupt it by keeping it. Danu keeps (harvest). The Shadow King keeps (undeath). The Morrígan releases. That is why she can run the valve and they cannot. The valve is the same virtue at the scale of two worlds that the last night is at the scale of two people.

The champion ethic

Saying yes to her is not submission-romance. It is the same strength she has --- holding love and death without renaming either. Cú Chulainn refused and still died. The prologue champion walks into morning because he can bear it. That is why she chose him. The seven-year hope Danu sells is the temptation to KEEP. The protagonist's early death is him recovering the champion-virtue: spend, don't hoard. Power does not change the contract. Love does not either. He can become the most powerful being in the universe and still be her champion.

The slow burn --- recognition, not announcement

  • Prologue: she spends a man. Reader reads predator. Sex is claiming, strategy, want. Leave it sharp. After the last page the scene is newly legible as love. It does not soften. The reader grows the eyes.
  • Y0--2: crow, whisper, weather, the dream. She does not appear as a rival girlfriend. She lets the vampires love him in the false way. She does not rescue him from the leash that pretends.
  • Near-misses only: washer at a ford, a woman on a night train, a crow on a stone he cannot pass. Every appearance readable two ways.
  • The Deal: he marries Danu's language. She is present and silent. She does not correct the seven-year wedding. Correcting it would be a seduction. She refuses the seduction.
  • After the cascade: everyone left offers comfort-as-possession. She offers the fact. She stays. She does not say I told you so. That is when he first suspects what she has been.
  • Last night: the burn completes. He understands she loved him the entire time and will collect him at dawn. He has the same strength. They bear it together. Sex is the treaty and the love and the briefing. Not a trick.
  • Last page, crow: she loved him. She spent him. She can carry that. She flies. The job continues. Not grief-porn. Not a smirk. Not a speech about the greater good. The reader who has walked the book already has the thesis.

Her faces

She can be one woman or three. For the prologue, pick a face and commit --- or let her shift across the night. Maiden is a trap. Sovereign is the one who negotiates the treaty. Crone-beautiful is the one who already knows the wound that will kill him. All three across one night is available and should be used at most twice in the book: prologue, finale.

Rules for writing her

  • Presence is not thesis. She can be on the page. Motive stays hidden until the last night. Plant consistency, not warmth.
  • She never lies. She omits. The distinction matters.
  • She does not rescue him. She spends him well.
  • Do not say the words. I love you is Danu's register and the nest's. Last night can be I will be there / you already knew. Crow-POV can hold the feeling without the sentence.
  • Never I wouldn't ask if I didn't love you. That collapses love into an excuse for the leash.
  • Do not put the thesis in a witch's mouth in year two. If anyone lectures true love lets go, the ending is spent.
  • Narration never sneers at the nest to make her look truer. Vampire loves stay sincere. They failed the losing test, not the feeling test.
  • One jealousy flicker, or save it for crow-POV. A subplot of competing for him turns her into a romance heroine in act two.
  • Sex grammar: tonight, morning, watch, bear, go. Time words. She never says stay. Never mine. Never harvest.

8. The Horned God and the seven-year deal

LOCK. Two clocks, only one advertised. Seven years is the expectation at the pledge --- his, and the reader's. His death is not seven years in. Do not signal that the clock can break. The prologue champion appeared to die when his death meant something; that pattern sells the idea that this man will get his full term. He will not.

What he signs

Danu's geis, spoken as sovereignty rite, not as mechanics: power now, seven years as consort and weapon, then he goes into the earth for her. He names a real end-date at the pledge --- a Samhain, a solstice. He makes a concrete plan that requires year six: a place, a person, a winter he thinks he will still have. The mid-tree lover(s) believe the date too. They plan a life inside his term.

What the pledge language must not say: that the Horned God-body is a vessel designed to ripen for seven years and then burst into the land. That bursting-vessel truth is what he understands on the last night. If the pledge explains it, a sharp reader guesses the ending.

What is actually running

The Horned God is not paid a weekly stipend. He is given a king-body --- the whole store starting to inhabit him, metered by the geis so it does not warp him to death in a week. Year 7 would have been a controlled release into Danu: a ripe king bursting into the Green hoard.

Morrígan's real contract, already taught in the prologue: she spends champions when the campaign requires it. She has never been a calendar. She did not promise him year 7. She promised him a meaningful death.

Three heresies, one office

  • The Shadow King / Slough Feg refused year 7 and fled into undeath. He stole time. He tried to make the term infinite.
  • A loyal king would have lain down on the named date and fed her.
  • This king does the third thing. He defects mid-term. He refuses the death she tries to collect. He spends the unused years against the lender.

How the early death works

Sequence, not simultaneous:

  • He serves Danu for a stretch after the pledge --- months to about eighteen months. He still counts toward year 7. He is her weapon. The lovers still believe in year six.
  • Before the root-kill, HIS lovers see the tree. He tells them, or the truth leaks. They know the kill will take them. They choose it. Willing and afraid, not serene. Last hours drop nest-grammar (mine, stay, keep) and pick up time-grammar (tonight, morning, go) without quoting the Morrígan. If there is sex it is farewell, not claiming. Do not sexualise their dying.
  • He kills the root as her champion. They help. They die. The rest of the line dies without a choice. Two rooms, same hour: close-shot grief, wide-shot horror.
  • THE CENTER. Sit in the grief. A bit of grief-porn is wanted here --- rooms, hands, bodies, cheap lager, the year-six object sitting on a table, the train they will not take. He is not noble. He is sick. He asked. They said yes. He survived. Narration does not call it necessary in the hour they die. In the hour it is just loss. Guilt stays specific once he can see: I put a real sacrifice on an incomplete altar.
  • Post-traumatic growth happens inside the grief, not after a chapter labelled Recovery. He stops bargaining for more time. He can look at a death he caused and not rename it. He can love the dead without trying to keep them --- Danu will offer to make their deaths feed the Green; refusing that comfort is the start of defection. The strength is capacity, not hardness. He is not colder. He is able.
  • The grief is what shows him the hoard, and why the second killing has to happen. If he stops at the root, he spent them for Danu. Their sacrifice must vent both ways.
  • He defects. Apostasy breaks the meter. The unripened store is still in him. The body starts coming due. Short window --- days, not months.
  • Danu smells the defection and tries to collect on her terms: a forced sacred-king death that would still feed her. She is not trying to murder him early. She is trying to keep him until the harvest date, and if she cannot, to harvest him herself. Two deaths are available.
  • Last night: Morrígan. This is the briefing and the slow-burn payoff. He did not know he could dump the term until she shows him. Hours, not months, after he understands. He also finally has the eyes for what she has been the entire time. The last fuck is treaty, love, and instruction in how to aim a bursting vessel. She still does not say stay.
  • He refuses Danu's death and spends the unused years the other way. The last warp-spasm is unlived time tearing through the Horned God-body --- antlers, heat, the years he will not have. Ugly before it is glorious. Bodily, not a mana bar. Then he is empty and he dies.

Conservation holds. He is not getting free power. He is detonating the loan. Cost is the unlived years plus the body. That is how a mid-term king kills a goddess.

Lived time of the novel

LOCK. About three to four years from the dream to the death. The deal around year two of the book. Death twelve to twenty-four months into the term. Pre-deal years (powerless / subservient / equal, the nest, the loves) do not shrink --- those loves have to be real before the cascade. After the pledge, accelerate. He expects a reign. He gets a war.

Where the deal is struck

A real threshold site. Strong candidates:

  • Newgrange / Brú na Bóinne at winter solstice --- womb of the Earth Goddess, tourist buses by day, the opposite at night.
  • The Hill of Tara --- kingship site, almost too on-the-nose, which can be used or avoided.
  • Oweynagat, Rathcroghan, Co. Roscommon --- the Morrígan's cave, a gate. Best if the deal is less clean than he thinks.
  • A lesser-known British overlay: Glastonbury Tor, Avebury, a locked estate in Wiltshire, a multi-storey car park in Birmingham that should not have a standing stone in the basement.

The body of the god

Warp-spasm is the Horned God beginning to inhabit him. Early: a shoulder that will not sit in its socket, heat coming off the skin, an eye that will not track. Mid: antler-buds under the scalp he can hide with a hood. Late: he does not fit in cars; CCTV does not know what it filmed; animals kneel or bolt. Final form is what dies, and it dies early, still unripe. Decide, before drafting, the exact line where the body-horror stops being titillating and starts being the cost. That line is the book's tone-signature.

Peek, then hide

The author was explicit: a lot of the Sláine material stays hidden at the outside, with a peek. The prologue is the peek. Then the book should read as modern vampire / urban-fantasy smut for a long stretch. Earth-goddess lore leaks through dreams, a standing stone, a witch who talks too much, a shifter who will not go near certain hills. The deal is the first time the Horned God is named in the present-tense narrative.

11. Prologue — beat sheet

Past tense. Last day only. Not the protagonist. Tone-set for the whole book: fucking, and bloody passionate intense battle, in the opening.

Afternoon

He already knows what tonight is. The whispering has been going on for months, maybe years. He is calm in the way of someone who has finished being afraid. Give him one mundane register so we are in modern Britain or Ireland immediately: a last pint, a cigarette behind a closed-down mill, a walk along a dual carriageway, a call he does not make.

Dusk --- the ford

She is washing something. Armour, or a shirt, or his own jacket. The water does not care. She looks up. This is the first time she is a woman to him, and not a voice. They fuck. It is not gentle. It is the Morrígan claiming a death. Sex is the treaty (Dagda at the Unius). He says yes with his body. She already had the yes.

Night

They do not sleep much. She can shift faces across the night if we want the triple. She tells him, without telling him, what the morning is for. One strategically essential thing. A death that means something. He understands enough.

Dawn --- the battle

A hidden-world fight that will look like something else in the paper: a riot, a dock fight, a deniable incident in the Sperrins, a knife-crime spike, a football firm that went too far. He enters the frenzy she gifts --- proto-warp-spasm, not yet the full Horned God, because he is a champion not a king. He takes many lives. He accomplishes the one thing. He goes down.

After

She watches from above in crow form. Lands on him at the last breath. The last image of the prologue is feathers and a mouth that has stopped asking.

Cut

Present tense. An unnamed man wakes. In the dream he was both the warrior and the crow. It is blurry and indistinct and fades from memory quickly. We get enough to understand he has dreamed the battle. We do not state that he is the next champion. The reader can read between the lines.

Do not explain the dream. Do not have him journal it. Do not have a helpful friend interpret it. Let it rot in him like a seed.

13. Sex and violence design

Principle

Sex moves power. Violence moves the war. When they happen in the same night --- prologue, certain nest scenes, the last night --- they are twins, not contrast.

Pacing

  • Main romance(s): slow-burn up the control ladder. Do not have the central pairing go all the way in chapter two.
  • Side stories: sex early and often, so the book is not Hannaford-slow. These can be tender, filthy, transactional, or magical. They should still change something --- a piece of information, a bond, a crack in the concealment.
  • Morrígan sex bookends the trilogy: prologue champion (Book 1), a mid-trilogy almost or vision (Book 2), the final night (Book 3). Three is the number. Do not add a fourth. The middle one is a near-miss --- the burn is not allowed to complete early.
  • Vampire sex is BIND. Blood and orgasm are the same gate. Grammar: mine, stay, again, don't leave, drink, keep. Ownership. Sincere.
  • Witch / nature / Danu sex is DRAW. Aether flood. Grammar: husband, land, ripe, years, harvest, I will make you more. Marriage. A lie about the end-date.
  • Morrígan sex is ENTER + WARP. Grammar: tonight, morning, watch, bear, go. Time. She never says stay. Tristan's law was that sex is where you are most deluded about the control you think you have. She is the exception the book earns. Sex with her is the one place control is named accurately. That is why it takes the whole novel to recognise it as love --- every other bed has trained the reader to hear love as a leash, so honesty sounds like coldness until the last night.

Power exchange and consent

Core themes, twin rails, sitting next to the leash line rather than under it. Power exchange is what the plot does with bodies. Consent is whether the mouth and the hour match. Love is whether anyone lies about the cost. The vampire years train him to treat all three as one word. The book spends the rest of its length unpicking them.

Some sex is a real exchange of power. Sometimes voluntary. Sometimes not.

The set-piece --- bound, made to come against his will

Once. Nest years. Subservient rung. Before the Deal. Binder: the Akkarin --- authority, the one who found him after the dream. BIND on the nervous system. Art / blood. Ropes or the equivalent. He can strain them. He cannot unmake the command. He hates it and is aroused by it. He does not want it. He comes. Both true. Stay in the mixed state: hate in the jaw, heat in the hip, the moment he knows he will come and still does not want to. Do not tidy it into secretly he wanted it. Do not tidy it into he is ruined forever. Those are the two lies the scene exists to refuse.

Aftermath sits. Shame, anger, the body's later flinch-and-lean. A hand on a wrist that brings the room back. Then he still has to get up and serve the house. That is the leash. No joke after. No fade-to-black.

Inverse of the last night: early, power is stolen from his body and someone else holds the end. Last night, he gives the body with a clear mouth and they hold the morning together. Same flesh. Opposite consent.

Who never does this

  • The Morrígan. She can. She doesn't. Binding him against his will would collapse want into use and spend the thesis. If she ever binds him it is because he asked and the morning is still named.
  • Danu's deal-night is a different violation --- yes to a lie about term, DRAW as flood. Do not put the same ropes on her harvest night. Echo is in the metaphysics, not the furniture.

Voluntary power exchange --- also required

  • He asks to be held down and means it. A stop exists and is honoured. Aftermath includes aftercare. Can be filthy and extreme. The difference is the stop.
  • Bully-to-lover owns this flavour: peer cruelty he could walk away from and doesn't. Hannaford lane. Tested, messy, human-scale even when the magic is on.
  • He holds someone who wants it. After he has power, at least one scene where he could force a body and does not. Temptation on the page. If he reaches for the command it costs him the same night. Repeating the set-piece as revenge spends the theme.

Four contracts, distinguished in bed

  • Nest early: desire manufactured or amplified. Stop-word fails or was never on the table. The set-piece lives here.
  • Bully-lover: stop exists, is tested, sometimes honoured. Voluntary PE.
  • Danu: he said yes to seven years. He did not say yes to the true harvest date. Consent to a lie.
  • Morrígan: yes to use, death, want, morning. The only complete yes.
  • Everyone sexually active is twenty or older. Hannaford energy without a school.

Violence

Passionate, close, costly. Warp-spasm violence is body-horror first and choreography second. Vampire violence is fast and intimate. Earth-side violence is older --- standing-stone, river, harvest. The prologue teaches the reader that a death can be glorious and still be a spending. Keep that standard. Random gore without strategy is not this Morrígan's register.

Three rooms of death --- all required, all different

He needs all three before he is allowed to spend himself.

  • Innocents. Did not choose. Not a vampire, not a champion, not willing. A night bus, a terrace street, a ferry queue, a Saturday market. Hidden war the papers call a gas main, a riot, a terror incident, a flood. Stay in one face, one name if he has one, one stupid detail --- a shopping bag, a phone still playing, a shoe. This horror cannot be metabolised into meaning, though he will try and Danu will help him try. Later it is the first thing he cannot forgive himself or her for.
  • Enemy-lovers. Chose the other side. Combat. Two of them, different movements, different aftertastes. Nest-side before the root-kill. Green-side after the defection. He has been in bed with them. The kill is not clean or cinematic. Stay in the recognition.
  • Willing lovers. Chose his side and the cost. Cascade. Already locked. Rehearsal of his end.

Horrors of war

No punches pulled means: stay with cost. It does not mean every chapter is atrocity. If the book is only atrocity the last night has no tenderness to stand on.

  • In the hour, narration is with his belief. Greater good is not ironic mid-battle. He and the reader want the Shadow King dead. We sign the bill together.
  • Warp-joy is part of the horror. The ríastrad feels like righteousness in the body. Next morning he can still see the street. Let him love the power in the hour and hate the bill after. That is how a champion gets pointed.
  • After a horror chapter, a small human scene. After victory, emptiness. After grief, a stupid ordinary thing that almost helps.
  • Default the on-page dead to adults. Children can exist at the edge of a street as the fact of a world he put holes in. Do not write dead children as a set-piece.
  • The Morrígan does not point him at civilians. She also does not lift him out of a war she did not start. After the reveal, the difference is visible: Danu metabolises the innocent dead into meaning. She does not. That is messy and correct, not clean and correct.

The second ledger

Manipulation does not mean there was no evil. The Shadow King is real. People in this realm were really being eaten. The lie is that one hoard's war was the whole good. Every "necessary" operation before the defection has a second ledger. He thinks: stop the nest, save the green, cut the root, protect a town. Actual: move Aether toward one hoard. When he sees the second ledger, greater-good language curdles. That is the change. Not a lecture. A re-reading of his own hands. I was a tool who loved the work.

14. Tense, POV, style

  • Prologue: past tense, close third on the champion, or a tight first if the voice is good enough to abandon. Recommendation: close third, so the cut into the protagonist's present-tense head is clean.
  • Novel proper: present tense, close third on the unnamed man. First person is available and would match Tristan's Tale and a lot of the smut sources; present-tense close third keeps the mythic register easier to lift when we need it.
  • Finale: present tense, close on the Morrígan in crow form. Short. Exact. No eulogy.
  • He stays unnamed for a while. When the name arrives, it should feel like a claim, not like the author got tired.
  • Register: modern British and Irish English, not mid-Atlantic. Swearing is fine. Mythic diction is earned in the presence of gods and not before.
  • Do not write tourist Ireland or heritage Britain. Write weather, class, counties, the way a night bus smells.

15. Setting — modern Britain and Ireland

The hidden world has always been here. It did not arrive.

Thin places that do mythic work

  • Newgrange / Brú na Bóinne --- womb, solstice, tourist car park by day.
  • Tara --- kingship, almost too obvious.
  • Emain Macha / Navan Fort --- Ulster Cycle ground.
  • Oweynagat, Rathcroghan --- her cave.
  • Giant's Causeway / Antrim coast --- a thinning that looks like geology.
  • Glastonbury / Somerset levels --- British Horned God overlay.
  • Avebury, Callanish, Tintern --- secondary wells.
  • London Underground / Crossrail --- Shadow-current architecture under old money.
  • A multi-storey car park that should not have a standing stone in the basement.
  • A pub on a crossroads that predates the Romans.

Mundane crucible

The Hannaford / Canavan bullying needs an adult box. Candidates: an elite graduate scheme, a City trainee house, a Dublin corporate set, a military mess, a vampire-kept artists' commune, a university department that is not for students. Pick one and let it be specific. Class has to bite. This book is partly about who gets to take and who gets taken.

Masquerade

The mundane world never quite sees it. A warp-spasm in an alley looks like a psychotic break. A Morrígan-battle looks like a knife-crime spike. A sídhe door looks like a locked estate. Do not hold a press conference. Do not have the Prime Minister briefed unless that is a Shadow-King joke.

Geography of the war

Vampires pool where money and old stone sit together: London, Edinburgh, Dublin Georgian, maybe Belfast as a contested thinning. Earth-current wells sit on the old ritual map. The motorway between them is not neutral. Ferries, bog roads, tube tunnels, and dusk at standing stones are crossings.

16. Themes

  • Simplification is the crime. Complexity is the good. Consent is how a complex system stays alive at the scale of a body.
  • Boost both realms. Extinguish neither. Kill the dictators. Leave the worlds. No third throne.
  • Whether love can tell the truth about cost. This is the personal-scale message. Everything else serves it.
  • Power exchange is the how. Consent is whether the how was agreed. Love is whether anyone lied about the cost. Unpick the three. Do not let a bed treat them as one word.
  • Arousal is not consent. Coming is not agreement. Hate and want can occupy the same hour.
  • She never pretends the leash is love. The leash is the job. The love is real and separate.
  • The only moral use of power is the one that can lose what it loves. Everything else in the book is a hoard.
  • Informed consent to being used --- the erotic and the tragic version of the same sentence.
  • Found family that is also a structure of extraction. Feeling sincere. Metaphysics false.
  • Two hoards calling themselves homes.
  • Glory as a form of spending.
  • A stolen future with a calendar face --- year six as a place and a person, not an abstract.
  • Three heresies, one office: steal time, pay on schedule, spend the remainder against the lender.
  • The body as a site the gods do not ask politely to occupy.
  • Balance as labour, not harmony. The crow does not rest.
  • Class. Who is battery, who is king, who only looks like either.
  • The strength to bear it --- hers, and her champions'. The slow burn of the whole epic is arriving at that as love.
  • Grief as rehearsal. The lovers' willing death is the first time the book shows the champion virtue in people who are not the Morrígan. He watches from the outside. Last night he has to have it from the inside.
  • Guilt that does not wipe clean because they said yes. Post-traumatic growth: capacity, not hardness. He is not colder. He is able.
  • Three rooms of death: innocents who did not choose; lovers who fight him; lovers who stand with him and die. He needs all three.
  • The greater good is sincere in the hour and revealed as manipulation later. Reader complicity. No mid-war irony.
  • Full emotional range at the extremes. Joy and tenderness have to exist or the horrors have nowhere to fall from.
  • Warp-joy: he likes the power in the hour. Then he has to live with having liked it.

The book is allowed to be sincerely filthy and sincerely interested in these at the same time. That combination is the point of synthesising Sláine with the smut sources rather than writing a sexless myth or a mythless fuck.

19. Open questions

These need answering before chapter one. Several can stay loose into a first outline; the ones marked urgent should not.

Urgent

  • Starting ground. London, Belfast, Dublin, rural west of Ireland, split between two, or somewhere less obvious (Manchester, Glasgow, Derry, a garrison town, a ferry port)?
  • Age and class. Twenty, mid-twenties, early thirties? Working-class, scholarship-into-elite, already inside the elite and failing at it?
  • One love interest or several. House Isador harem (capped at two or three, not twelve) versus a single Akkarin figure versus one vampire and one witch.
  • The bully. Nest-mate, human crucible, or witch?
  • How visible is the supernatural. Full masquerade, leaking masquerade, or open-secret among people who know not to say?
  • Single novel or a trilogy dressed as one epic. Lived time is now 3--4 years, which helps a single volume. A trilogy still maps onto team-vamp / team-goddess / apostasy, but risks losing the "one life, one geis" feeling.
  • Exact end-date named at the pledge. Which Samhain or solstice does he speak aloud? Which year-six promise (place, person, winter) do the lovers believe?
  • Heat register. Literotica-open (anatomical, long scenes, little fade-to-black) or dark-romance-literary (explicit but cut tighter)? This decision changes chapter length and who the book is for.

Can wait for outline

  • His name, and who is allowed to use it.
  • Akkarin gender and exact role (sire, house-head, professor, something else).
  • Which thin place hosts the deal.
  • Which real geography hosts the prologue battle.
  • Whether a dragon appears at all.
  • Whether skinwalker rules exist.
  • Whether the four treasures of the tribes appear as objects or only as echoes.
  • Final working title.
