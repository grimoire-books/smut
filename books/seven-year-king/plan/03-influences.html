3. Influences

From Planning Bible · Draft 07. Canonical whole: PLANNING-BIBLE.md. Word source: material/source/Seven_Year_King_Planning_Bible.docx.

Locked decisions are marked LOCK. Open choices are marked OPEN.

Each source is treated as a parts bin, not a template. The finished book should not be recognisable as a remix of any one of them.

Sláine: The Horned God --- Pat Mills / Simon Bisley

The mythic frame of the entire novel. A Celtic warrior becomes consort to the Earth Goddess Danu and incarnation of Carnun, the Horned God. The Sessair king reigns exactly seven years, then is ritually killed so his earth-power returns to the land. Slough Feg is the previous Horned God who refused to die, ate Time Worm eggs to prolong life, and rotted into a tyrant. Danu is already not purely good in the source: playful, cruel, fond of word-games, approving of ritual death because "death is only a pause."

Steal

  • Seven-year sacred kingship as a job, not a punishment.
  • The king as husband of the land. Sex with the goddess or her avatar is a sovereignty rite.
  • Warp-spasm / ríastrad: earth-power warping the body from the inside. Ugly. Monstrous. Not fully under his control. First triggered, in Sláine, by childhood bullying.
  • Slough Feg as the template for the Shadow King: the Horned God who said no.
  • Danu's moral slipperiness, held back until the late turn so the reader loves her first.
  • Matriarchal theology. He serves. He does not rule the goddess.

Discard

  • Tír na nÓg as the primary setting. This book is modern Britain and Ireland with the Otherworld alongside it.
  • Ukko the dwarf, Brainbiter as a named axe, the four tribal treasures as a collectathon --- usable later as texture, not plot engine.
  • Front-loading the Horned God. After the prologue peek, this material stays buried until the deal.

The Morrígan, Cú Chulainn, sacral kingship

Phantom Queen. War, fate, sovereignty. Triple aspect: Badb (crow, panic, prophecy), Macha (the land's right to choose its king), Nemain (the frenzy that drives men mad mid-fight). She does not swing a sword. She whispers into hearts and spends lives like a general spends battalions.

Before the Second Battle of Mag Tuired she couples with the Dagda at the river Unius and only then commits her strategy to his side. Sex is the treaty, not the reward. She offered herself to Cú Chulainn; he refused; she became his enemy in animal forms; when he died lashed to a standing stone she landed on his shoulder as a crow. That is the last word of their relationship.

Irish sacral kingship (Frazer, Mac Cana, the bog bodies): the king is spouse of the territory. Fixed term. Death is excessive by design so the sacrifice feeds the goddess in all her aspects. Nipple-cutting on bog kings was dethronement --- sucking a king's nipples was a gesture of submission; destroying them un-kinged him in this world and the next.

Steal

  • Whisper first, appear as a woman only on the final night, fuck, watch the death as a crow.
  • The Washer at the Ford, used in the prologue and again on the last night.
  • Oweynagat (Cave of the Cats, Rathcroghan) as her house and a gate.
  • Geis as the legal form of the seven-year deal.
  • The inversion of Cú Chulainn: these champions say yes. Outcome is the same. Meaning is not.

Discard

  • Morrígan-as-romantic-lead. She is not the girlfriend. She is the only being who tells him the truth, and the truth is always you will die and I will be there.
  • A textbook dump of the Ulster Cycle. One Irish-myth name early (hers). The rest earned.

Tristan's Tale --- IncomingPornDuck

Unfinished Literotica epic (12 parts, then deleted). Mind-control smut that grows into a secondary-world war. Readers stayed for the characters and the scale, not just the kink. The Art is mental magic working from the inside of a mind; Chi is the self externalised; Castles are shared lucid-dream constructs where practitioners meet, hide, fight, and rewrite people. Sex sits on the line between reptilian instinct and conscious intention --- the best attack surface because it is where people are most deluded about the control they think they have. The antagonist plants ideas in a culture until they become policy. LOTR-scale enemy that feels unbeatable because they work through people.

Steal

  • Mind-work as ENTER, not puppetry. You meet a mind in clay / the between and rewrite from inside. Cost is real: you risk your own Castle.
  • Sex as the gate between instinct and will.
  • The big bad as an idea-planter, not only a monster.
  • Giving the tools to more than one person so the book is not a one-way control fantasy.
  • He is controlled first. Then he learns the Art. Then he refuses to use it the way it was used on him. Then he uses it once at the end as chosen sacrifice.

Discard

  • The secondary-world isekai door-in-the-house. Our Otherworld is already here.
  • Unexamined non-con as the book's standing temperature. Early violation is diegetic and later answered.

Endangered --- ltpc, Literotica

A hidden dragon in modern America, raised by a witch, whose aspect is the Ether itself: "the nothing between everything," a sub-level of reality where everything in the Norm has a bleed-through shadow. Dragons overflow with magic. Sex can break concealment spells and trigger emergence. High-end work includes pocket-space, pressure and heat, matter creation. Witchcraft is attached to nature and is expensive to take into the Ether.

Steal

  • Clay / the between as medium. The protagonist's aspect is the between, not green and not shadow. That is why both sides want him. Aether-as-fuel is superseded.
  • Exceptional draw from the other realm, beyond what is normal.
  • Sex as the thing that cracks the hiding-spell on his nature.
  • Witch magic as land-work, slower and costlier than his raw draw.

Discard

  • Dragons as a species, the American farm, the harem-of-magical-women logistics, explicit dragon-cock transformation as the book's identity. If a dragon appears it is one being, late, and earned.

Eragon --- Inheritance Cycle

Magic is energy work. Cost equals the real-world physical work of the same act. Overdraw kills. Language is a focus tool, not the source; wordless magic exists and is more dangerous. True Names grant control over the named thing. Knowing your own True Name is self-mastery.

Steal

  • Conservation of energy as hard law. No free lunch. You pay with life, borrowed life, or landscape-life.
  • Wordless magic as advanced and unsafe.
  • True Name as a late-game identity beat: he learns his name as balance, not as either faction's champion.

Discard

  • A constructed language the reader must learn. Dragons as partners. Elves.

Black Magician trilogy --- Trudi Canavan

Sonea, slum-girl, bullied inside an elite magical guild. Akkarin, High Lord, looks like the villain for two books: he takes her as ward and hostage to protect a forbidden practice that is actually holding back an invasion. Higher/Black Magic means dropping the polite visualisation of a personal "source" and siphoning life-energy from other living beings. Willing gift versus theft is the moral line. She offers herself as a battery so he can stay alive. Love arrives late, after hate and fear. He dies. She finishes the fight with the last of his power.

Steal

  • The Akkarin figure for the team-vamp phase: older, grey, apparently the problem, actually fighting a war he will not name.
  • Protagonist starts as Sonea (bullied, hostage, battery) and becomes Akkarin (the one who holds the forbidden technique).
  • Systematic humiliation that does not break him; it forges control. The bully can later flip or stay as a wound.
  • Power-sharing as intimacy.

Discard

  • The Guild-as-boarding-school. Teenage students. A capital-city court of magicians. Anyone sexually active in this book is twenty or older.

Hannaford Prep --- J. Bree

Scholarship outsider at an elite school. Already a weapon ("the Wolf") pretending to be prey. The rich boys bully her; the slow burn turns them into lovers. Violence is real. Found family is the prize. The humiliation is a tactic she endures because blowing her cover would cost more than the bruises.

Steal

  • Already lethal, choosing to eat the humiliation. Do not write a helpless lamb. Write a weapon that has agreed, for now, to be sheathed.
  • Bully-to-lovers as one strand, not the whole book.
  • Found family that will be tested by the cascade.
  • Slow burn on the main attachments; the user is right that Hannaford is a bit too slow --- side-stories carry the heat early.

Discard

  • The school. The underage setting. Import the energy into an adult crucible: graduate scheme, City house, military mess, vampire-kept commune, elite Dublin or London set.

House Isador, Forged in Blood, Twilight

House Isador: a vampire queen and her Blood. Taking someone is love, feeding, mental access and power-share in one act. Devotion is both the kink and the politics. One existing Blood is already "the Morrígan's own Shadow" --- useful proof the two mythologies can share a universe.

Forged in Blood: the exact control ladder requested. Prey and abduction; blood-bond dressed as protection; possessive intimacy; he becomes the fulcrum; the bond is revealed as a leash belonging to someone older. Dark academia texture. A professor-sire who can read minds is a clean first-act Akkarin.

Twilight, stripped for parts: a human trait amplified into a unique mental gift on turning; a shield as late-game answer to the Art; shifter pack-mind and an Alpha voice that can force obedience.

Steal

  • The taking economy. Sex and blood are the same act.
  • The five-stage control ladder as the spine of the relationships.
  • Sire-line death cascade on TVD/Originals physics: only the root dying collapses the tree. Intermediate sires dying does nothing.
  • Amplified mental gifts. One shield.

Discard

  • Isis/Triune chess-court and a twelve-person harem. Sparkle, vegetarian moral high ground, imprint-as-destiny, YA chastity. Vampires in this book should feel like wet earth, old blood, and borrowed time.

True Blood + Stranger Things

Shifters: born, not bitten. First change under a hard moon. Any animal once imprinted; each person has a go-to form that costs almost nothing. Full moon is involuntary. Sleep dumps them back to human. Packs and territory. They stay secret longer than vampires.

Stranger Things contributes only the two-realms image: a world alongside this one, leaking, hungry, with a thing at the centre of it. Celtic cosmology already has this (the Otherworld is horizontal, not vertical) and is the better source. Do not steal the Demogorgon, the kids-on-bikes register, or Upside-Down-as-rot-copy.

Steal

  • Shifter rules as written above.
  • Three political slivers among the packs: land/green, city/shadow, Morrígan crow-kin.
  • The Otherworld as adjacent, entered by threshold not distance.

Discard

  • Coming-out-to-the-press vampire integration. Skinwalkers except as one villain, once.
