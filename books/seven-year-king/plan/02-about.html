2. About

From Planning Bible · Draft 07. Canonical whole: PLANNING-BIBLE.md. Word source: material/source/Seven_Year_King_Planning_Bible.docx.

Locked decisions are marked LOCK. Open choices are marked OPEN.

On the surface: sex, violence, hidden gods, a man becoming a monster and then a god and then a corpse. Some of the sex is a real exchange of power --- sometimes he asks, sometimes he does not get to.

Underneath: whether love can tell the truth about cost. Every major relationship in the book is a negotiation about who holds the leash. The vampires dress the collar as found family. Danu dresses the harvest as marriage. The Shadow King dresses hunger as home. The Morrígan never pretends the leash is love. The leash is the job. The love is real and separate. She can hold both. That is her strength, and it is the strength she requires of her champions.

She is the messy, morally correct centre of the book. Messy at the scale of a body --- people she loves die, and she watches. Correct at the scale of two realms --- neither hoard wins, both worlds keep existing. She does what the greater good requires, always. She is the only big deity figure who actually loves persons. She sacrifices what she loves with her eyes open. She has the strength to bear it. So do the ones who say yes.

This is the ultimate slow burn. It takes the whole epic to see it. If the reader finishes the prologue thinking she is the villain, good. If they spend the nest years thinking Danu will be the beloved, good. The thesis arrives when he does --- on the last night --- and not as a speech. Sex and violence are the surface. This is what everything is built toward.

The moral engine is not good versus evil as team jerseys. Simplification is the crime. Complexity is the good. Consent is how a complex system stays alive at the scale of a body. Two dictators are trying to flatten a living two-realm system into one reservoir. He kills the dictators, not the worlds. He dies so he is not the third. The crow continues the work without sitting a throne.

Deep structure --- the undercurrent

This is not surface plot. This is what the plot is doing when it thinks it is doing something else.

  • Good = structuring and balancing a complex system. Messy. Many agents. Feedback. Both realms fed and loud. Boost both. Extinguish neither.
  • Bad = unbalancing and destroying that complexity. Collapsing it toward one pole, one will, one reservoir.
  • The two extremes that call themselves good and bad are the same crime in different flags. Danu simplifies into summer. The Shadow King simplifies into night. Each is a dictatorial authority. Each turned a realm into a monopoly.
  • The shadow realm was only turned to evil. It is supposed to be the other lung --- source, winter, death-that-feeds-return, the far side of the valve.
  • By the end we realise the material realm was turned too. Danu's life is a dictatorship of summer. Harvest dressed as health.
  • Real good lives in three conditions: no ultimate dictatorial authority; free consensual exchanges; a complex system that can hold a lot of both.
  • Same thesis at three scales. Body: forced orgasm is one will taking a climax; last night is two wills naming a morning. War: innocents and enemy-lovers are a system being simplified by a campaign. Cosmos: Aether drawn only one way is a realm being starved.
  • It is a trilogy. Part 1 he is shadow. Part 2 he is Earth Goddess. Part 3 he is Morrígan --- in ethic and allegiance, not in office. She outlasts him.

Tone contract

  • Epic fantasy scale (realms, gods, a war that predates cities) crashing constantly into banal modern Britain and Ireland: trains, wet streets, cheap lager, a Greggs at dawn after a killing.
  • Smut that is load-bearing, not garnish. Sex is how power moves. If a sex scene could be cut without changing who holds the leash, it is a side-story and should be labelled as such.
  • Body-horror sitting next to heat. The warp-spasm is not a hot super-mode. It is the Horned God arriving in a human body that was not built for it. The reader should want him and be a little afraid of wanting him.
  • Violence that is passionate, not procedural. The prologue sets this: battle as sex's twin, not its opposite.
  • Slow-burn main attachments; frequent side-sex so the book never goes cold waiting for the romance to ripen.
  • Full range of human emotion at the extremes. The book is allowed to be funny and horny and tender on Tuesday and unendurable on Thursday. If every chapter after year one is grim there are no extremes --- only one note, loud.
  • No punches pulled on cost. Stay in the room. Not a catalogue of atrocities. Few innocents, few enemy-lovers, go all the way down.
